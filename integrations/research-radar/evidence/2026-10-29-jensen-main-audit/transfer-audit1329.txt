Exact retained hypotheses.
M is full row rank and unimodular in the source's maximal-minor convention; A is integer-valued with E||A|| finite; endpoint fibers P(A)={x>=0:Mx=A} are nonempty almost surely; b=EA is integral; P(b) is nonempty bounded and has actual affine dimension at most3. This is not a theorem for every rank-deficient matrix under a vacuous minor convention, arbitrary nonintegrable demands, or all higher-dimensional fibers.

Uniform endpoint control and the common active face.
Nonempty central boundedness rules out any nonzero z>=0 with Mz=0, since x+t*z would be a central ray. On the compact nonnegative unit l1 simplex, ||Mx|| therefore has positive minimum eta. Scaling gives ||x||_1<=eta^-1||Mx|| for every x>=0. Thus every feasible endpoint fiber is bounded and every selection of an endpoint point is integrable. For n=0 the feasible count is1 and the conclusion is immediate.

Integer demands have countable support. Let J be the central coordinates positive somewhere. Suppose a point in the fiber of a positive-probability atom had a positive coordinate outside J. Select it at that atom and arbitrary feasible points at every other atom. Countability makes this selection measurable, and the uniform estimate makes it integrable. Its expectation is a nonnegative central point with that coordinate positive, contradicting J. Hence all points of all positive-probability endpoint fibers lie in the same coordinate face. This argument is for every point at every such atom, not just for one arbitrary selection. A finite average of central witnesses is positive on all J, so the affine hull on that face is the entire retained solution affine space. Consequently r=|J|-rank(M_J)=dim aff P(b)<=3; no assumption on the full kernel dimension of M is substituted.

One lattice for the whole demand law.
Choose a full column basis B0 of M, with determinant +/-1. Its inverse is integral. The normalized [I,D0]=B0^-1M is TU: each minor of D0 completes with identity columns to a maximal minor, and minors of [I,D0] reduce to such minors. Restrict to J, choose s independent rows, and choose s pivot columns H of determinant +/-1. This fixed row/column choice is independent of the demand atom. Write the retained equations as H*x_pivot+D*z=c(a), with c(a) integer linear in a. At feasible demands omitted equations are consistent linear combinations, so they impose no missing constraints on these solutions.

With C=H^-1D the parameterization is x_pivot=H^-1c(a)-Cz, x_free=z. Every integer z gives integer x, and every integer x gives exactly its integer free coordinates. There is no finite-index lattice relaxation. Normalizing the TU full-row-rank pivot system as above makes C TU; stacking C with signed identity rows preserves TU. The nonnegativity constraints are Cz<=H^-1c(a), -Iz<=0. Sign changes used to classify row directions do not reverse actual inequalities: the final signed-root set restores their original signs and offsets. Rows with zero normal remain feasibility conditions; they may be ignored only at the already feasible demands used in the theorem.

For r<3, add coordinates fixed to zero using both opposite inequalities. This preserves TU and the lattice bijection, including r=0. The padded normal matrix contains I3 up to row signs. The exact finite classification1323 supplies one GL(3,Z) right transformation taking all fixed normals to signed A3 roots or zero. Its determinant and all51valid systems/16maximal systems were checked then; no new classifier run is needed. That one transformation applies to every demand atom and the mean. Padding may produce lower-dimensional root polytopes; the audited A3 extension includes those exact boundaries.

Fixed graph, finite paths and no hidden support cap.
Represent each signed root w_i-w_j<=c(a) as edge j->i, retaining parallel edges. The fixed four-node graph is strongly connected: otherwise its acyclic condensation admits a nonconstant potential nonincreasing along edges; subtract the anchor value to get a nonzero recession direction for the bounded central transformed polytope. For each ordered pair use the minimum over its finitely many simple directed paths. Strong connectivity makes those path lists nonempty. All path lengths are integer linear forms in a, so h(a) is integral at integer a, componentwise concave as a minimum of finitely many linear forms, and satisfies ||h(a)||<=C||a|| for one fixed finite C. Parallel edges do not create an infinite list because simple paths have at most three edges on four nodes.

At feasible demands there are no negative cycles. Original inequalities imply every path inequality, while each direct edge is among the paths; hence the transformed polytope equals P_h(a). Zero-normal feasibility conditions already hold there. The equality is asserted only at feasible demands; it is not silently extended to infeasible a. Since Y=h(A) is integrable and feasible, it lies in the A3 feasible closed convex polyhedron D almost surely. For each path p, E h_ij(A)<=E c_p(A)=c_p(b). Taking the finite minimum yields EY<=h(b). This uses one fixed graph/lattice, not a choice changing with each atom.

Countable Jensen without assuming its conclusion.
Let G be the nonnegative monotone concave A3 extension audited1324-1328. Enumerate Y's atoms y_s with masses p_s. For a tail of positive mass r_k, its conditional mean m_k exists by Y integrability and belongs to D: every finite cycle inequality is preserved by expectation. The finite mixture of the first k atoms and this one tail mean has mean EY. Finite concavity gives G(EY)>=sum_(s<=k)p_sG(y_s)+r_kG(m_k)>=sum_(s<=k)p_sG(y_s). An empty tail is omitted. Monotone convergence proves E G(Y)<=G(EY)<infinity; integrability of G(Y) is a conclusion, not a prerequisite. An interior mean or finite support is not required.

Monotonicity and exact lattice values now give N(b)^(1/6)=G(h(b))>=G(EY)>=E G(Y)=E N(A)^(1/6). Nonempty bounded unimodular fibers contain lattice vertices, so every N(A) is a positive integer. Therefore0<=log N(A)<=6N(A)^(1/6) is integrable. Scalar logarithmic Jensen yields E log N(A)<=6log E N(A)^(1/6)<=log N(b). Both source conclusions and finiteness claims follow under the retained hypotheses.
