1. Exact boundary values, conditional on the chamber representation.
Assume a fixed closed rational parameter cone has bounded nonempty lattice polytopes P_a at integral parameters; assume P_(ra+sv)=rP_a+sP_v for nonnegative r,s, and a fixed polynomial F equals the count at all integral strict-interior parameters. Choose integral a in that cone and integral strict v with full-dimensional P_v. Then a positive v coefficient makes ra+sv strict whenever r>=0,s>=1. Polynomiality supplies E(r,s)=#((rP_a+sP_v) intersect Z^3) for every nonnegative integral r,s, including s=0. Thus E(r,s)-F(ra+sv) vanishes on all r>=0,s>=1. For each positive integer s its polynomial in r has infinitely many roots; each coefficient polynomial in s then has infinitely many roots. The difference is identically zero. Evaluate at(1,0): #P_a=F(a). This uses polynomial identity, not continuity of lattice counts. Degenerate P_a and a point P_a are allowed. Essential hypotheses retained: common lattice, exact Minkowski equality, strict-parameter coverage, and the actual count-polynomial identity. None is supplied by a numerical grid.

2. Why a common chamber may give its Minkowski equality.
For strict parameters, suppose fixed feasible bases give linear vertex maps and a common normal fan. At each common normal cone, the maximizing vertex map is linear in the bounds, so support functions add; equality of all support functions gives P_(a+b)=P_a+P_b. Extension to cone closures can use the finite vertex maps, not count continuity. For example, x in boundary P_a and y in strict P_v give x+epsilon*y in P_(a+epsilon*v); finite convex vertex representations and a convergent subsequence of their coefficients recover the boundary convex hull as epsilon decreases to zero. This argument remains conditional on the claimed complete feasible-basis/chamber description. The six-seed chamber enumeration and volume/count coefficients have not been checked here.

3. Raw directed bounds retain their variables.
On the feasible domain (no negative directed cycles), shortest-path closure c(h) is a minimum of finitely many simple-path linear forms. It is continuous, integral at integer h, componentwise concave and monotone. Summing the original inequalities along paths gives P_h contained in P_c(h); direct edges give c(h)<=h and the reverse inclusion. If G0 on tight supports is concave and coordinatewise nondecreasing, then c(sum lambda*h)>=sum lambda*c(h) gives G0(c(sum lambda*h))>=G0(sum lambda*c(h))>=sum lambda*G0(c(h)). This is an all-parameter conditional mechanism, not a finite test. Monotonicity cannot be omitted.

4. Conditional monotonicity of the proposed count expression.
Retain F=V+Q+L+1 with V the actual Euclidean volume, Q the global quadratic and L=sum(a_ij)/3-sum|g_s(a)|/12. For comparable feasible supports, V is inclusion-monotone. The12 exact gradient/triangle-slack identities checked1324 give nonnegative derivatives of Q throughout the convex tight-support cone. Each coordinate occurs in exactly two g_s forms, so a nonnegative increment d changes the sum of their absolute values by at most2sum d; consequently L increases by at least(sum d)/6. This handles wall crossings without assigning a derivative to an absolute value at zero. In the potential gauge a_i4=0 all coordinates are nonnegative by triangle/two-cycle inequalities. There Q=(a dot gradQ)/2>=0 and L>=sum(a)/6>=0, hence F>=1. Gauge invariance follows from the translation kernel of B, the zero row/column sums of g_s, and translation invariance of actual volume. The representation by actual V and the fixed chamber count polynomial remains a hypothesis; isotonicity alone does not certify it or prove chamber concavity.
