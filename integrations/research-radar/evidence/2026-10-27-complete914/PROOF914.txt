Proposed structural theorem914, 2026-10-27. Own working proof, not reviewed.
No manuscript score, priority, formal verification or capacity theorem claimed.
This is a NEW TARGET, not a proof of the earlier non-GAD entropy inequality.
All spaces are finite-dimensional; all entropies use natural logarithms.

1. Definitions and proposed general statement

For a positive definite S and Hermitian Y define the positive-cone BKM form
 g_S(Y)=integral_0^infty Tr[Y(S+tI)^(-1)Y(S+tI)^(-1)] dt,
and ||Y||_S=sqrt(g_S(Y)). This definition does not require TrY=0 or TrS=1.
For semidefinite S use the form on its support when Y is supported there.
Ad_F means X -> F X F, with F positive definite.

Let M,N: L(A)->L(B) be CPTP and M(I_A)>0. Suppose Q:L(B)->L(B) is CP,
trace-nonincreasing, and Q^k=0 for an integer k>=1. Assume that a>=0,c>0,
an invertible positive F, and m>0 obey

 Q o M <=_cp c M,           F^2 >= m I_B,
 N = Ad_F o (id+aQ) o M.                                  (H)

Define P_k=sum_(j=0)^(k-1)(a sqrt(c))^j and

 L=m / [(1+ac) P_k^2] >0.                                 (L)

PROPOSED THEOREM. For EVERY finite reference R, and every pair of density
operators rho,sigma on A tensor R with supp(rho) subset supp(sigma),

 D((N tensor id_R)rho || (N tensor id_R)sigma)
 >= L D((M tensor id_R)rho || (M tensor id_R)sigma).          (T)

All assumptions in(H) are finite linear-map/Choi/algebraic conditions on
M,N,Q,F; none is an assumed entropy inequality on arbitrary references.
No smallness condition a sqrt(c)<1 is imposed. Nilpotency supplies a finite
inverse. No sharpness of L or tensor-power multiplicativity is asserted.
The full-output-support assumption M(I)>0 is explicit and retained.

2. BKM facts, with the nontraceless issue made explicit

(i) CPTP monotonicity extends to every Hermitian tangent Y. For TrS=1 write
Y=tS+Y0, TrY0=0. Since g_S(S,Y0)=TrY0=0 and g_S(S)=TrS=1, the trace component
contributes t^2 on both sides of a CPTP map. Ordinary tangent monotonicity,
obtained by differentiating relative-entropy DPI, handles Y0. Scaling S
handles non-unit trace. Consequently a CP trace-nonincreasing map Q obeys
 g_Q(S)(QY) <= g_S(Y):
extend Q to a CPTP map by appending a one-dimensional failure flag with
weight Tr[(I-Q^*(I))S], and drop the nonnegative failure-block BKM term.
For full-rank S, QY is supported in Q(S) because -dS<=Y<=dS for some d.
The same reasoning applies to Q tensor id_R; the appended flag may discard
the reference in its failure branch, which does not affect the inequality.

(ii) If U<=bV for b>0, then g_U(Y)>=b^(-1)g_V(Y), with the support convention.
One proof uses U+tI<=bV+tI, inverse order and the substitution t=b u.
For positive A>=B, Tr(Y A Y A)>=Tr(Y B Y B): on vectorization,
A^T tensor A-B^T tensor B=(A^T-B^T)tensor A+B^T tensor(A-B)>=0.
This also justifies the resolvent comparison for noncommuting matrices.
Singular U follows by a positive regularization on the relevant support.

(iii) Congruence estimate: if F^2>=mI, then
 g_FSF(FYF) >= m g_S(Y).                                  (C)
Indeed the integrand becomes Tr[Y(S+t F^(-2))^(-1)Y(S+t F^(-2))^(-1)].
Since F^(-2)<=m^(-1)I, inverse order plus the tensor argument in(ii) bounds
it below by the integrand with S+(t/m)I. Integration gives(C).
This is a metric comparison for an unnormalized filter; it is NOT an
unjustified use of channel DPI for a non-trace-preserving congruence.

3. Proof of the proposed general theorem

First take a full-rank density sigma on A tensor R. Abbreviate tensor id_R
on all maps, F tensor I_R by F, S=M(sigma)>0 and Y=M(X), X Hermitian.
Full rank follows from sigma>=epsilon I and M(I)>0. CP order in(H) gives
 Q(S)<=cS, so by(ii) and(i), for EVERY Hermitian Z,

 g_S(QZ)<=c g_Q(S)(QZ)<=c g_S(Z).

Thus the operator norm of Q in the Hilbert norm ||.||_S is at most sqrt(c).
This assertion is used on nontraceless Z as well; item(i) is essential.
Nilpotency gives the exact inverse
 (id+aQ)^(-1)=sum_(j=0)^(k-1)(-aQ)^j.
If Z=(id+aQ)Y, the triangle inequality implies
 ||Y||_S <= P_k ||Z||_S,
 g_S(Z)>=P_k^(-2)g_S(Y).                                 (I)

Meanwhile positivity and CP order give
 N(sigma)=F(S+aQ(S))F <=(1+ac)FSF,
 N(X)=FZF.
Using(ii), then(C), then(I),

 g_N(sigma)(N(X))
 >=(1+ac)^(-1)g_FSF(FZF)
 >=m(1+ac)^(-1)g_S(Z)
 >=L g_M(sigma)(M(X)).                                  (BKM)

This proof is unchanged in every finite reference dimension: CP order,
trace-nonincreasing complete positivity, nilpotency and m survive tensoring.
It does not reduce the reference to a qubit or a classical register.

For full-rank rho,sigma put X=rho-sigma, sigma_t=sigma+tX. The exact Hessian
identity D(rho||sigma)=integral_0^1(1-t)g_sigma_t(X)dt, also after either
channel, integrates(BKM) to(T). For the stated singular pairs regularize
both with the same full-rank omega=I/(dimA dimR). For each output pair,
joint convexity supplies an upper bound (1-epsilon) times its limiting
finite relative entropy, and lower semicontinuity supplies the matching
lower limit. Input support inclusion guarantees finite output relative
entropies. Therefore(T) passes to epsilon down to zero.

4. Application to two qubit amplitude-damping channels

For 0<p1<p2<1 set s_i=1-p_i, d=p2-p1. The channel A_p is
 A_p([[U,C],[C*,V]])=[[U+pV,sqrt(1-p)C],[sqrt(1-p)C*,(1-p)V]],
where entries may themselves be arbitrary reference operators.
Let M=A_p1, N=A_p2, Q(X)=|0><1| X |1><0|, so Q^2=0 and Q is CP TNI.
Choose
 F=diag(1,sqrt(s2/s1)), a=d/s1, c=s1/p1, m=s2/s1.
The exact map identity N=Ad_F(id+aQ)M follows on all four matrix units.
Moreover, if K0=diag(1,sqrt(s1)),
 c M-QM=c Ad_K0 is CP, because QM=s1 Q and M=Ad_K0+p1 Q.
Thus all hypotheses(H) are verified, with no unknown reference assumption.

The proposed complete relative-entropy lower bound has coefficient

 L_AD = [p1 s2/(p2 s1)] / [1+d/sqrt(p1 s1)]^2.            (AD)

For example p1=1/4,p2=1/2 yields L_AD=1-sqrt(3)/2>0.
At p1=0 or p2=1 no positive uniform coefficient is asserted. Equal parameters
give identity comparison separately. The scalar coefficient p1 s2/(p2 s1)
without the extra denominator is already the ORDINARY exact expansion
coefficient claimed in Han--Li2610.29373v1 Theorem4. We do not claim its
ordinary formula as new or prove its sharp complete extension.

5. A finite-height example beyond qubits and beyond a small-perturbation bound

On a qutrit let J=|0><1|+|1><2|, Q=Ad_J, Q^3=0. For b>0 define
 D_b=diag(1,(1+b)^(-1/2),(1+b+b^2)^(-1/2)),
 M_b=(id+bQ+b^2Q^2) Ad_D_b.
The Kraus D_b,sqrt(b)J D_b,b J^2 D_b satisfy trace preservation exactly.
M_b(I)>0 and (1/b)M_b-QM_b=(1/b)Ad_D_b is CP.
For 0<a<1 let
 F_a=diag(1,sqrt(1-a),sqrt(1-a+a^2)),
 K_a=Ad_F_a(id+aQ), N=K_a M_b.
K_a is CPTP since F_a^2+a J* F_a^2 J=I. Thus(H) holds with c=1/b,
k=3,m=1-a. It gives a complete relative-entropy coefficient
 (1-a)/[(1+a/b)(1+a/sqrt(b)+a^2/b)^2]>0.
At b=1/100,a=1/2 this is1/98022, although a sqrt(c)=5>1. A Neumann-series
small-norm argument would not justify an inverse here; finite nilpotency does.
This is a concrete structural example, not a capacity-additivity claim.

6. Complete mutual-information consequence, and an important obstruction

Define eta_MI^cb(N,M)=sup_rho_AR I(R:B_N)/I(R:B_M), and the corresponding
infimum coefficient check_eta_MI^cb when the denominator is nonzero.
These use one input state and its product of marginals, not channel
relative entropy between two different channels applied to different inputs.
Taking sigma=rho_A tensor rho_R in(T) gives
 I(R:B_N)>=(L) I(R:B_M) for every finite R. In particular(AD) is a strictly
positive lower bound for check_eta_MI^cb(A_p2,A_p1). No sharp value is claimed.

However, for every fixed 0<p<1 the following separate proposed equality holds:

 eta_MI^cb(A_p,id)=1.                                     (O)

It is crucial not to substitute the ordinary strict contraction coefficient
for this complete mutual-information coefficient in a capacity application.
Here is a two-qubit-reference witness for(O), not a reference-dimension limit.
For x,r in(0,1/2) set
 S_xr=diag(1-x,x) tensor diag(1-r,r), X=sigma_x tensor sigma_x,
 rho_epsilon=S_xr+epsilon X, with 0<epsilon<lambda_min(S_xr).
Both marginals equal those of S_xr. Data processing gives the upper bound1.
For epsilon down to zero the ratio of output to input mutual informations
converges to the ratio of BKM forms of X at the corresponding product bases.
Let ell(a,b)=(log a-log b)/(a-b), continuously ell(a,a)=1/a. With s=1-p,
u=1-sx,v=sx, the two forms, including all off-diagonal entries, are

 g_in=2[ell((1-x)(1-r),xr)+ell((1-x)r,x(1-r))],
 g_out=2s[ell(u(1-r),vr)+ell(ur,v(1-r))].

First let r down to zero at fixed x. Each ell contributes log(1/r) divided
by its nonvanishing endpoint, giving
 g_out/g_in -> s[(1/u)+(1/v)]/[(1/(1-x))+(1/x)]
               =(1-x)/(1-sx).
Then x down to zero gives1. The limits are iterated: choose x, then r,
then sufficiently small epsilon; every witness is a positive finite state.
No approximation of finite entropy by a nonuniform Taylor expansion is
asserted across the limit; epsilon is taken first for each fixed product base.
At p=1 the channel is constant and eta_MI^cb=0; that endpoint is excluded.

A completely explicit rational sequence removes the need for iterated
choices of epsilon. For integers n>=3 put x_n=1/n, r_n=2^(-n^2),
epsilon_n=r_n/n^2 and rho_n=S_(x_n,r_n)+epsilon_n X. All matrix entries
are rational, and lambda_min(S)=r_n/n, so
 (1-1/n)S <= rho_n <= (1+1/n)S.
CPTP maps preserve these inequalities. Applying metric order along the
segment S+t epsilon_n X gives, for input and output separately,
 epsilon_n^2 g_S(X)/[2(1+1/n)] <= D(rho_n||S)
 <= epsilon_n^2 g_S(X)/[2(1-1/n)],
with the corresponding output base/form in the output expression.
Thus the finite mutual-information ratio is at least
 [(1-1/n)/(1+1/n)] times the BKM ratio at that product base.
In the displayed log-mean formula, -log r_n=n^2 log2 dominates log x_n;
the second term is asymptotic to n^3 log2/s before the output prefactor s,
and to n^3 log2 at input. Both total forms are asymptotic to2n^3 log2.
The BKM ratio and hence the finite mutual-information ratio tend to1.
This establishes a single explicit sequence, not a uniform Taylor assumption.

In fact every rho_n is SEPARABLE but not classical on R. Set
D_A=diag(sqrt(1-x),sqrt(x)), D_R=diag(sqrt(1-r),sqrt(r)) and
t=epsilon/sqrt(x(1-x)r(1-r)), with 0<t<1. Then
 rho=(D_A tensor D_R)[I_4+t sigma_x tensor sigma_x](D_A tensor D_R).
The middle operator is sum_(a,b=+/-1)(1+tab) P_a tensor P_b, where P_a
are sigma_x eigenprojectors. Positive coefficients and local filtering
give an explicit separable cone decomposition. The R marginal has distinct
eigenvalues1-r,r and [rho,I tensor rho_R]!=0, so rho cannot be classical
on R in any basis. Entanglement is therefore not necessary for the
complete-vs-classical auxiliary contraction separation, if this proof holds.

For an amplitude-damping main channel with damping gamma in(0,1/2),
its degrading map has damping (1-2gamma)/(1-gamma) in(0,1). Thus(O), if
correct, makes the factor
1-eta_MI^cb(degrader,id) equal zero in the sufficient construction discussed
in BGSW v3 Section7. Positivity of our relative expansion bound alone does
NOT establish their desired informationally degradable nondegradable example.
It does not prove the old fixed non-GAD Holevo/capacity target either.

7. Prior-art and hypothesis gate

The new proposed mechanism is finite inverse control of a nilpotent CP
noise map in the BKM norm, combined with CP domination on the channel image
and a congruence bound. It explicitly handles the operator argument as well
as the base state; CP order alone is insufficient, as BGSW stress.
The eliminated hypothesis is perturbative smallness / an unverified
restriction on finite quantum-reference dimension in these comparisons.
Full output support, nilpotency, TNI and CP image domination remain.
