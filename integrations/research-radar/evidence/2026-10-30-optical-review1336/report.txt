MATHEMATICAL ASSESSMENT OF THE COMPLETE PAPER

1. Uniform classical comparison and compound coding (Sections 1-4, pages 1-5).

The measure-prepare identity subtracts |a|^2 thermal photons, not one, and the heterodyne input outcome adds exactly one photon per mode. Its refinement through a classical coherent-state preparation variable preserves a single decision kernel for every fixed unknown mixing law. This is the correct reason entangled codewords and collective quantum receivers are covered by the converse. The hidden preparation variable is not given to the real receiver.

I checked the characteristic-function factors, normalized plus/minus variances, and the constants in (1). With E >= 2K/v_*, the minus floor is v_*/2. Noise sensitivity costs 2/(v_* E) for the plus channel and 4K/(v_* E) for the minus channel. Concavity in the cost then contributes log(1+K/v_*)/E. No quantum additivity theorem is being assumed for the non-Gaussian mixture.

The entropy-continuity lemma has the necessary density upper bound as well as a second-moment bound; its tail estimate prevents entropy from escaping to infinity. The score/heat-flow proof first works for bounded centers and then truncates the input, avoiding unjustified differentiation for arbitrary heavy-tailed input. The classical direct theorem supplies the common decoder: finite input approximation, compact-family/Dini output quantization, constant-composition threshold decoding, and a polynomial channel net. The net sum of expected errors is used correctly; there is no expected-supremum interchange. Cost slack is kept until the finite approximations and type selection are fixed. I found no missing channel-knowledge assumption in this reduction.

2. Gaussian hull and Gaussian-average functionals (Section 5, pages 6-9).

The moment-annihilating radial perturbation is valid because the binary flag entropy is bounded, positive at finite radius, and decays at infinity, hence is not affine in squared radius. Inequality (11) has the correct sign and quadratic KL remainder. Injectivity of the signal/reset output map gives strict input concavity. The proof does not stop at showing that one circular Gaussian is suboptimal: it uses strict growth and compact attainment of the Gaussian scheduling hull to exclude arbitrary displacement, anisotropy, singular covariance, and non-message-bearing time sharing. Sublinear information growth in cost is what makes the hull objective continuous on its compact space of parameter laws.

The bounds for Gaussian average quantum states use their Gaussian Husimi marginals and correctly combine costs before applying the hull. They cover conditional non-Gaussian states and correlated Gaussian averages. The lower construction for R_G uses rational finite schedules of coherent Gaussian modulations, whose product average is Gaussian. This supports the matching functional limit. The paper appropriately does not turn this regularized Holevo functional into an operational capacity of finite books with exactly Gaussian averages.

3. Robust marginals and typical books (Section 6, pages 9-13).

The common-submeasure proof of (19) is sound even when the supplied d is only an upper bound on total variation: one can deliberately shrink the common part to mass 1-d. This avoids a false monotonicity inference for h_2(d). The exceptional components have inflated cost P/d; the information estimate can be proved by branch revelation without subtracting infinite mixed differential entropies. The perspective/Jensen step in (20) handles very unequal individual modal energies.

The typical-book proof separately controls trace distance and empirical first/second moments. This separation is essential and its counterexample is correct. The finite-rank fluctuation bound and the exponential message count make the union bounds summable over modes and lengths. The common rescaling has vanishing average whole-block trace perturbation, so it transfers an already proved decoding reliability statement without creating one. The operational corollary uses Fano at fixed E and preserves the distinction between typicality and existence of a reliable decoder. It does not establish an exact capacity for all protocols called Gaussian, and I have not interpreted it that way.

4. Assistance and feedback (Section 7, pages 13-15).

The reference-preserving lemma is the important step. I checked the complete measure-prepare representation with a retained reference and the identity I(M;Y|R)=I(MR;Y)-I(R;Y) <= I(X;Y). The bound makes both right-hand mutual informations finite. Receiver operations and copied feedback transcripts do not supply free forward information. In the feedback converse, the actual law can affect input costs through the history; Jensen is applied separately under each actual law and its expected budget. In the no-feedback converse, the averaged input law stays common to the family. These are the correct and different optimization orders.

The finite-family direct theorem estimates a finite kernel index with a sublinear pilot and chooses a payload book whose cost is feasible even when the estimate is wrong. The vacuum pilot in Proposition 13 has the displayed Bernoulli probabilities and Hoeffding exponent. It costs zero transmitted input photons, not zero channel uses or zero total apparatus/environment energy.

The old capacity-matched family chooses b^2=r(exp(C1)-1). Its strict gap follows from compact attainment plus the unique AWGN maximizer, not merely from different formal optimizers. Neither its capacity nor its numerical gap is computed. The explicit family in Section 8 is different: b^2=9757/50000 and C1>C2>G1. I did not transfer a numerical bound between these two families.

5. New explicit pair (Section 8, pages 15-20).

The radial likelihood ratio and density factors in (28) are correct. For T=|Y-2sqrt(z)|^2/2, signal and reset have the two noncentral Gamma densities displayed, including the signal factor two. The exact cross-product definition of h preserves mass and energy symbolically. The rounded printed h values are not used as definitions. The scalar balls imply the coarse bounds in (29), and the exact lower expression .17825+.005*.0078-.005^2*.739 equals .178270525, strictly above C2.

The weighted-observable lemma is correct for every cost-one input, without centering, radiality, bounded support or a fourth-moment assumption. The AWGN deficit is D(mu W2||Q2)+b^2(1-P)/(1+b^2), hence dominates the divergence. Each integrated residual costs at most 2 eta; the difference costs 4 eta. Natural-log Pinsker gives ||P-Q||_1 <= sqrt(2D), with L1 twice total variation. Thus d <= delta+M sqrt(2 delta), and the rationalized lower root in the lemma is correct. This uniform inequality, rather than a single-law improvement or a distance between optimizers, is what quantifies the common-input deficit.

Completing the Gaussian square and averaging the angular Bessel representation gives E[g(Y2)|x]=F(|x|^2), with the coefficients and factors in (30). The function in z is entire through 0F1, so no global square-root branch is assumed. The real-axis bound |g|<=sum |c_j| is valid even though this norm is very large.

I traced both holomorphic majorants. The improved M_H follows from the positive 0F1 series and its first moment; the factor 2Z/k^3 is included. It is not silently substituted for the coarser broad-box integration oracle. The logarithm arguments stay in a safe right half-plane on the specified strips; the actual callback propagates the analytic flags and rejects inadmissible analytic boxes. The broad-box rectangle encloses the holomorphic finite-t integral. Integration tolerances are goals, not proofs of the returned radius.

For first-region ellipses, the imaginary semiaxis is 3/16, and the first leftmost real coordinate is -1/16, so both the logarithm strip and the pole at z=-1 are handled. The Bessel complex norm bound follows from |Im sqrt(z)|^2=(|z|-Re z)/2. DCT constants correspond to first-kind roots, not extrema. Root-grid aliasing maps each high mode to zero or a signed low mode; the manuscript's interpolation allowance is conservative. In the second region only F is interpolated, so the wider ellipses do not incorrectly apply H's strip. The t-tail follows from the stated exponential moments and posterior entropy <= log 2. The Bhattacharyya bound and binary entropy inequality give the decaying B_H. Both final tail envelopes decrease. I found no analytic gap in this route from finite balls to all z >= 0.

The resource conclusion follows with A+B=6+log 2. The exact rational check of (d0-r0)^2>2 M0^2 r0 and d0>r0 is positive for d0=5.333e-6, M0=1.5e20 and r0=6e-52. The same zero-input-photon pilot identifies the explicit pair because the gain disappears at vacuum while the noise mixture remains distinguishable. At E=1e53 the positive first term multiplied by E is 60, whereas 6+log 2<7. The displayed finite-energy separation is therefore valid under the certified premises and the earlier operational model. This is an asymptotic coding statement at each fixed finite E, not a finite-block performance guarantee.

NUMBERED OBJECTIONS AND RESERVATIONS

O1. Minor reproducibility improvement: the portable verify-package1335.py intentionally checks archive integrity, declared status and coverage; it does not itself parse every bound/scalar inequality or link every worker row back to the mathematical assertions. The separate runner does those checks during execution, and my checks of the frozen records passed. A short read-only numerical-record verifier should also check the stored ball endpoints, coefficient-table equality, scalar779 threshold and exact final rational inequality without repeating integration. That would make the advertised inspection route more useful to a new reader. This is not a finding that the current 320 bounds fail.

O2. Nonblocking provenance limitation: the recorded runtime hashes cover python.exe, flint __init__.py and arb.pyd. They do not hash acb.pyd or demonstrate a complete closure of the loaded FLINT/Arb shared-library dependencies. Thus the records establish a versioned, source-bound computation with selected backend observations, not a fully pinned reproducible binary environment. For future executions, preserve the relevant wheel/package lock and complex integration/shared-library hashes. Do not retroactively label newly collected hashes as evidence of the old process. The manuscript's present trusted-library caveat is necessary and should remain.

O3. Substantive significance reservation: neither changing sup-inf to inf-sup through finite-family learning nor non-Gaussian advantages for fading are new general mechanisms. Primary antecedents confirm this. The explicit bound quantifies one deliberately constructed pair and its size is dominated by a huge inverse-observable norm; it is not an estimate of the actual gap, a usable energy requirement, or a new optimal modulation theorem. The paper mostly says this correctly. Its scientific value should be defended through the uniform bosonic comparison and the sharply specified Gaussian/marginal class, not the dramatic resource headline alone. No numerical or editorial score for a different artifact resolves this reservation.

O4. Minor exposition: the abstract is dense, the numerical section is interleaved with long internal provenance identifiers, and Section 10 plus parts of Section 9 devote substantial main-text space to prior model-assessment history. Put most historical audit detail in a supplement while keeping the exact artifact links, AI disclosure, adverse findings and trust boundary accessible. A compact dependency table would help readers distinguish analytic theorem, fixed scalar premises, full320 records and operational consequence. Correct the visible 'Consequently Writing' at page 16 before (28), and use 'zero transmitted-photon pilot' consistently to avoid suggesting zero time or zero environmental energy. No major layout defect was visible in the inspected renderings.

These objections do not currently identify a false theorem. O1 and O4 motivate the minor-revision recommendation; O2 and O3 are persistent limits on reproducibility and significance, not reasons to manufacture a new campaign or a new review score.
