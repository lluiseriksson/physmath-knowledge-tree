3. Claim-by-claim scientific assessment

3.1 Definitions and the source assertion (PDF pp. 1-2)
The later-on-the-left convention agrees with source2607.01446v1 Appendix A equations (A.1)-(A.6). The definitions of first and eventual site-count lengths agree with (A.2)-(A.3). In source Lemma A.1 on p. 34 the pointwise-adjoint family is driven by the same forward T; the source explicitly asserts both almost-sure equalities. Merely reversing labels leaves the time arguments on the wrong factors. The manuscript displays the discrepancy correctly. It does not attack an identity that secretly uses the inverse base.

The unnormalized output-first convention J(Phi)=sum Phi(E_ab) tensor E_ab is consistent in the PDF. For a completely positive map, J is positive semidefinite and its rank equals Kraus-span dimension, independently of Kraus representation. Thus positive definiteness of J is exactly the full-span test. The autonomous exception is correct.

3.2 Two- and three-phase counterexamples (Theorems 1-2, pp. 2-3)
DUD=L, D^2=D, and U^2=id follow exactly from the dephasing projections and the Hadamard matrix. All local channels in both examples are trace preserving and unital, with both normalizations equal to I. Their Hilbert-Schmidt adjoints are faithful as well. A uniform finite cycle is invertible, measure preserving, and ergodic, although not mixing; no mixing assumption is needed for the contradicted source lemma.

For the two-cycle, the original first/eventual onsets are (3,2), while the same-forward-base adjoint onsets are (2,3). In phase 0 the original prefix is D, UD, L; in phase 1 it is UD, L. The adjoint prefixes are D, L and DU, DU, L. The pre-onset rank is 2 and J(L)=I_4/2 has rank 4. Each later unital map fixes L. This proves the all-length claims, beyond the finite computations. Both phases violate the claimed same-point equality, while the two distributions happen to agree.

For the three-cycle D, UD, UDU, the original onsets are (4,3,2) and the forward-adjoint onsets are (2,2,2). The listed products reduce to UD before the appropriate depolarizing block; every consecutive adjoint two-step product is L. This genuinely separates the distributions: at length 2 the probabilities are 1/3 and 1. The stronger bistochastic and two-sided-faithful hypotheses are satisfied, so the objection cannot be dismissed as a normalization or singular-support pathology. No minimality or uniqueness of these examples has been established or claimed.

3.3 Correct reversal and onset laws (Proposition 3 and Theorem 4, pp. 3-4)
With inverse base and local matrices A_i(T^-1 omega)^*, the reversed word is A_in(T^-n omega)^* ... A_i1(T^-1 omega)^*. Taking the adjoint of the original shifted word with reversed labels gives exactly this expression. Therefore W_hat_n(omega)=W_n(T^-n omega)^*, and E_hat_n=T^n E_n. Invertibility and measure preservation suffice for equality of fixed-block distributions; ergodicity, normalization and faithfulness are not used.

The shift depends on n. The displayed unions demonstrate why the individual probability equalities alone cannot identify a first-onset law. Under dual faithfulness, F=sum A_i A_i^*>0 gives sum_i A_i M_d=M_d and hence persistence under a later left factor. Under original faithfulness, G=sum A_i^* A_i>0 gives the corresponding persistence for the reversed local adjoints. Both matrix decompositions of Y in the proof are valid with the displayed order of inverses. Countably many integer shifts give a common full-measure set. Thus both full-span events are increasing, first and eventual times agree, and their distribution functions agree at every integer, including the mass at infinity. Source Lemma A.3 already contains the persistence ingredient and is appropriately credited.

The three-cycle's genuine reverse has onsets (4,2,3), so even under these stronger hypotheses equality in law is not same-base-point equality. The caveat that this algebraic reversal need not be a trace-preserving physical reverse outside the bistochastic case is appropriate. Both faithfulness assumptions are sufficient; the paper does not prove that they are the weakest possible assumptions.

3.4 Reset obstruction (Section 4, p. 4)
Both R and B satisfy sum K_i^*K_i=I. The reset map sends a to Tr(a)E_11, has singular output on I, and its adjoint is not faithful. The stated subspace identities QV=M_2, VM_2=V, and VQ=V hold. The original ranks are 2,4,2,4,... in phase 0 and 2,2,4,2,4,... in phase 1. Thus first times are (2,3) and eventual times are both infinite.

For the genuine reversal the local spans are Q^* and C. The identities C Q^*=M_2, Q^*M_2=M_2, Q^*C=C, CC=C hold; additionally CM_2=M_2 supplies the other persistence step. Phase 0 is full from length 2 onward; phase 1 stays C forever. Hence both reversed times are (2,infinity). Each fixed n>=2 has full-span probability 1/2 in both directions, despite different first and eventual laws. I independently checked these subspace identities and 24 phase/length cases through length 12. The all-length conclusion follows from the identities, not those samples. This is a correct obstruction to dropping persistence, not a counterexample to Proposition 3.

3.5 Conditional Choi-rank convergence (Theorem 5, p. 5)
The proof is valid and does not smuggle in ergodicity, invertibility, an exponential rate, or a uniform spectral floor. The estimate ||J(Psi)||_infinity <= d^2 ||Psi||_(1->1) follows by expanding all d^2 matrix units, using ||E_ab||_1=1 and ||Psi(E_ab)||_infinity<=||Psi(E_ab)||_1. Its nonoptimal constant is harmless. J(Delta_z)=z tensor I under the specified convention.

If the last map has singular Phi(I), all its Kraus ranges lie in a proper output subspace, so every composition ending in it has a nonzero Choi kernel. A unit kernel vector then yields the lower bound f(T^n omega)<=a_n(omega) on that event. This is an output-support argument, not the invalid common right-kernel assertion attacked later. It needs no measurable kernel-vector choice.

For g=1_B f circ T the inequality 0<=g circ T^(n-1)<=a_n holds. Since a_n tends to zero almost surely, the left-hand side tends to zero in probability. Its distribution is independent of n by measure preservation. Therefore g=0 almost surely, and f circ T>0 makes B null. Only forward pullbacks of this null exception are needed to obtain local positive output at every future step. There is no independence or mixing assumption here.

The level sets A_k={f>=1/k} cover a full-measure set. Poincare recurrence for a probability-preserving map, including a noninvertible and nonergodic map, gives infinitely many future returns for almost every point in each A_k. Removing countably many exceptional sets is sufficient. Each remaining starting point belongs to some A_k; after a_n is below 1/(2k), a later return makes J_n positive definite. The choice of k is trajectory dependent, which is why no deterministic lower bound on f is required.

Finally J_(n+1)=(Phi_(T^n omega) tensor id)J_n >= eta Phi_(T^n omega)(I) tensor I>0 gives all later full ranks. The first positive smallest Choi eigenvalue defines a measurable finite onset; finite-time maps and their eigenvalues are measurable. On the exceptional null set this can be assigned infinity. Fixed finite dimension and complete positivity are essential to the stated argument; d=1 presents no special failure. The conclusion is an almost-sure finite random onset, not a deterministic bound or a rate for that onset.

3.6 Forced trace preservation (Remark 6, p. 6)
The warning is correct and important. Apply convergence at omega and T omega to the cocycle identity Phi_omega^(n+1)=Phi_(T omega)^(n) Phi_omega. Taking traces of the two approximations, whose target states both have trace 1, bounds |Tr Phi_omega(a)-Tr a| by [e_n(T omega)||Phi_omega||+e_(n+1)(omega)]||a||_1. On a common full-measure set the bracket tends to zero. The estimate holds simultaneously for all complex matrices by the operator norms. Thus the hypothesis itself forces local trace preservation. The theorem must not be sold as covering arbitrary normalized non-TP CP products, and the manuscript explicitly avoids that overclaim.

3.7 Repair of source Proposition A.9 (Corollary 7, p. 6)
The easy implication is correct: a positive-definite Choi matrix gives positive expectation against every nonzero rank-one input and output, hence strict positivity. Consequently q<=ell pointwise. The converse uses the actual source reference [18], Movassagh-Schenker 1909.11769v4, rather than an unrelated or weaker mixing claim.

The source's standing setup on pp. 5-6 is a measurable CP-map-valued family over an invertible, ergodic, probability-preserving transformation. Assumption 1 is eventual strict positivity of phi_N ... phi_0. The manuscript's q<infinity is exactly that assertion after converting site count n to endpoint N=n-1. Additional dual faithfulness is not being silently postulated: the source's Lemma 2.1 derives it as part of the equivalent conditions. The cited result has no extra moment hypothesis that is omitted here.

The source Theorem 1 supplies faithful trace-one Z and Z', with Z'_j=I/d in the trace-preserving case. Theorem 2, equation (2.10), uses the induced trace norm on all complex matrices and normalizes Psi_(b,a) by Tr[Psi_(b,a)^*(I)]. For a trace-preserving block this scalar is d, not 1. The rank-one approximant is Tr(Z'_a a_input) Z_b=(Tr a_input/d) Z_b. Thus multiplying the entire normalized estimate by d recovers the ordinary replacement channel, with prefactor d C_(r,0).

The manuscript correctly identifies its length-n block as source Psi_(n-1,0), uses source endpoints a=0, b=n-1, x=0 for n>=2, and therefore gets exponent n-1. Source Z_b=z^MS_(T^(n-1) omega), so tilde_z_omega=z^MS_(T^-1 omega) makes this equal to tilde_z_(T^n omega). This shifted density field is stationary and faithful. No missing extra factor of 2 is required: Theorem 2's C already belongs to its all-complex-matrix operator norm. Omitting the first length is immaterial to convergence. Theorem 5 then supplies eventual full Kraus span without invoking source Lemmas A.1, A.6 or A.8.

There is a small quantifier issue in the presentation: the source guarantees the existence of a rate r in (0,1), and its proof chooses r above its contraction exponent kappa. The bare '0<r<1' after equation (3) should explicitly mean such an admissible fixed rate, not every r in that interval. Read with the preceding invocation, the intended existence statement is justified. An arbitrary-rate reading would be false: for Phi=p id+(1-p)L on a one-point base, a traceless input gives an error at least p^n, which no finite prefactor can bound by r^(n-1) when 0<r<p. This wording issue does not obstruct the repaired finiteness implication, which needs only convergence.

The common-kernel criticism of the source is exact. A proper Kraus span does not imply a nonzero vector annihilated by every Kraus matrix; {I_2} already refutes the inference. More strongly, for any trace-preserving tuple, sum K_i^*K_i=I rules out a common nonzero right-kernel vector. The source's p. 37 step is therefore invalid under its own normalization. Replacing it by the Choi-kernel argument is appropriate. This demonstrates a proof error, not falsity of Proposition A.9. No conclusion about the main source's parent-Hamiltonian or spectral-gap theorems follows from this review.
