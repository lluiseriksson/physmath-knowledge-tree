1. RADIAL REDUCTION — CHECKED

Write x=sqrt(z) real, z>=0, since the flag problem is rotationally covariant. The signal and reset conditional densities are phi_1(y-x) and phi_2(y), with equal prior weights. Their likelihood ratio is

  phi_1(y-x)/phi_2(y)
     = 2 exp(-|y-x|^2+|y|^2/2)
     = 2 exp(z-|y-2x|^2/2).

Thus L=log2+z-T, T=|Y-2x|^2/2. The posterior flag probability is a measurable function of T; its entropy conditioned on Y equals its entropy conditioned on T. This establishes sufficiency for this binary flag at each fixed x. It does not identify T as a sufficient statistic for arbitrary communication messages.

Under signal, Y=x+G_1, so (Y-2x)/sqrt(2) is a complex Gaussian of mean -x/sqrt(2) and complex variance1/2. Under reset, (Y-2x)/sqrt(2) is a complex Gaussian of mean -sqrt(2)x and variance1. Polar integration, or expansion of the noncentral squared-magnitude density, gives

  f_s(t,z)=2 exp(-2t-z) sum_{j>=0}(2zt)^j/(j!)^2,
  f_r(t,z)=exp(-t-2z) sum_{j>=0}(2zt)^j/(j!)^2.

The signal prefactor2 is the radial scale/Jacobian factor and is necessary. More explicitly, signal is a Poisson(z) mixture of Gamma(j+1,scale1/2), whose j-th term is

  exp(-z) z^j/j! * [2^(j+1)t^j exp(-2t)/j!].

Reset is a Poisson(2z) mixture of Gamma(j+1,scale1). Their displayed products give exactly the two densities. Nonnegative terms allow Tonelli for t,z>=0. They integrate to one. Total expectation/variance then gives signal mean(1+z)/2, variance(1+2z)/4, and reset mean1+2z, variance1+4z. No factor-of-two or Poisson-parameter discrepancy was found.

With equal priors, conditional flag entropy is the integral of

  (f_s/2) log(1+exp(-L)) + (f_r/2) log(1+exp(L)).

Substitution gives exactly F(t,z) in780 and both Python scripts, including the factor1/2 only on the displayed reset exponential term after the common 0F1 factor is taken out. The function called hypgeom_0f1(1) is the ordinary 0F1(;1;argument); the parameter is1 and the default is unregularized. At parameter1 the gamma normalization would in any event be1, but the installed wrapper's documented argument convention was checked directly.

For X~CN(0,1), z has density exp(-z), not an additional z or 2pi factor. The matched output is CN(0,2), and the binary flag identity gives

  h(Y)=log(2 pi e),
  h(Y|X)=log(pi e)+(3/2)log2-E H(z),
  G1=E H(z)-(1/2)log2=J(0,infinity)-(1/2)log2.

The sign and constant in certificate779 are correct.

2. HOLOMORPHIC ENCLOSURE AND ARB CONTRACT — CHECKED

The key proof obligation is an enclosure of the entire requested complex box. Agreement at real sample points or repetition at a higher precision cannot replace it.

2.1 Holomorphy. For real t in[0,200] and |Im z|<1/4, each exponential inside a logarithm has argument magnitude<1/4. Its real part is positive. Therefore 1+that exponential has real part>1, is nonzero, and lies away from the principal-log cut. The factor 0F1(;1;2zt) is entire in z and t, directly from its power series. On a finite t interval, integration preserves holomorphy in the z strip, by uniform domination on compact subsets. H_T(z) is thus the holomorphic function being enclosed. No square root of complex z is evaluated by the implementation.

2.2 Uniform magnitude. Let Z>=sup|z| and ell<=inf Re z for the entire box. The positive coefficients imply

  |0F1(;1;2zt)| <= sum_j (2Zt)^j/(j!)^2
                 <= exp(2 sqrt(2Zt)) <= exp(t/2+4Z).

The middle inequality can be proved directly from binomial(2j,j)<=4^j and the positive even terms of exp(2sqrt(u)); it does not require a branch choice for the evaluated complex function. The last inequality is arithmetic–geometric mean.

For either logarithm, Re(log) is nonnegative, its argument has magnitude<1/4, and log(1+A)<=max(log A,0)+log2 for real A>0. Using |Re z|<=Z and t>=0 bounds the logarithm's modulus by t+Z+3. For example the first real part is at most log(1+exp(t-Re z)/2); its imaginary part is at most1/4. The second logarithm obeys a still smaller estimate. Thus the absolute integrand is bounded by

  exp(4Z-ell) exp(-3t/2)(t+Z+3)
     +(1/2)exp(4Z-2ell)exp(-t/2)(t+Z+3).

Integrating this positive bound to infinity, which also bounds[0,200], yields exactly

  M=exp(4Z-ell)[4/9+2(Z+3)/3]+exp(4Z-2ell)(Z+5).

Consequently every H_T(z) in the requested box has real and imaginary parts in[-M,M]. This validates rough(z). The bound is loose, but looseness affects work/accuracy rather than correctness. A ball-valued enclosure rule need not itself be an analytic formula in the box midpoint/radius; it must enclose a function proved holomorphic on that box. The coarse rule satisfies that requirement.

2.3 Outward bounds. abs(z).upper() is an outward scalar upper bound; z.real.lower() is a lower bound. The arithmetic constructing M is ball arithmetic, and the final M.upper() is upward rounded. The constructor arb(0,M) adds an enclosing radius, as confirmed by the version-pinned wrapper source. The returned rectangle therefore contains the required disk. No midpoint-only substitution is made.

2.4 Narrow boxes and flags. H first rejects any box for which |Im z|<1/4 cannot be certified. Broad boxes use the proved bound. On narrow boxes, the inner integrand retains the full z ball. For an analytic t request it rejects t boxes not wholly within |Im t|<1/4. Combined with the z strip, both exponential argument angles have magnitude<1/2, so the logarithms are holomorphic. Both logs receive analytic_t or analytic_z. Any interval overestimation that touches a forbidden cut can cause a non-finite enclosure and subdivision rather than an unchecked branch value. 0F1 with parameter1 has no singular parameter or branch cut to test.

The nested inner quadrature encloses the integral for every parameter z in the box because its integrand enclosures and quadrature bounds are uniform over that parameter ball. The outer call then encloses exp(-z)H_T(z). Neither the threshold1e-18 selecting a method nor a change of adaptive subdivision discards interval radii.

2.5 API contract. The installed python-flint0.9.0 documentation and version-tagged acb.pyx were inspected. The wrapper maps the callback's analytic flag to the underlying order1 holomorphic-domain request. Its integral method returns the computed enclosure; it does not return the C convergence status. The Arb integration documentation states that tolerance values are goals and cumulative error can exceed them. Therefore certificate779 correctly decides with the returned balls, not with abs_tol, requested precision, or printed digit count. A finite result with a wider-than-requested radius is not by itself a failed enclosure. The code's finite/imaginary-zero-containment and strict interval comparisons are the relevant checks.
