1. Quantum mutual information, not accessible information
The paragraph in proposed Theorem C saying the bottleneck increases Bob's
"accessible information" must be read as quantum mutual information I(M;R).
The latter is the quantity in the displayed chain-rule identities and the
telescope. No inequality about increments of optimized accessible information
has been proved. This clarification follows a wording objection raised in
the sole ongoing technical audit745; its original objection must be retained.

2. An explicit zero-cost pilot for the two-law pair (author derivation)
The audit745 initial response noted that vacuum probes already distinguish
the pair. Here is an explicit classical decision rule and error calculation.
This addendum is not itself a new independent review or a manuscript grade.
Put u_E=r+1/E and w_E=s+1/E, s=k+r, and use t vacuum channel inputs.
Heterodyne and normalize the outcomes by sqrt(E). For each probe let
Z=1{|Y|^2<=u_E}. Under the Gaussian law2,
 p2=P(Z=1)=1-exp(-1).
Under matched-reset law1,
 p1=lambda(1-exp(-1))+q(1-exp(-u_E/w_E)).
Consequently p2-p1=Delta_E=q(exp(-u_E/w_E)-exp(-1))>0.
Use threshold (p1+p2)/2 for the empirical mean of the t independent bits.
Each conditional error is at most exp(-t Delta_E^2/2) by Hoeffding's
one-sided inequality. The pilot has zero transmitted photon cost.
Bob feeds its one-bit decision to Alice; both then select the corresponding
payload codebook. Wrong estimates still obey the same physical payload cost.
At fixed E, t=ceil(A log m), any A>0, gives a vanishing estimation error and
t/m->0. No uniform payload coding length in E, numerical capacity gap, or
finite-length optimality claim is made.
