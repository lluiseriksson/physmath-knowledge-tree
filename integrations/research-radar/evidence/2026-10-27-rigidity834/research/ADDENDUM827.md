## Multiplicative rigidity

Assuming Lemma A of823, let k>=2 and let Q,P_1,...,P_k be nonempty lattice polytopes in the same ambient integer lattice. If product_i E_{P_i}(z)=E_Q(z)^k as Laurent polynomials over C, then there are integer vectors h_i with P_i=Q+h_i and sum_i h_i=0. Indeed, each nonunit irreducible factor of E_Q appears k times on the right. Each of the k squarefree factors E_{P_i} contributes at most once, so all contribute once. No other irreducible factor may occur. Thus E_{P_i}=c_i z^{h_i}E_Q; all coefficients are1, forcing c_i=1. Equality of monomial units gives sum_i h_i=0. Conversely these translations imply the identity. Monomial enumerators cause no exception. This is a polynomial-identity assertion, not a coefficientwise inequality or an arbitrary Jensen assertion at one positive field.

## Strongly polynomial arithmetic operation count, conditional on823B

The weak polynomial bound in823C can be strengthened with a standard LP theorem, without asserting a new LP algorithm. Let M in Z^{r x n} have full row rank and every nonzero maximal minor +-1. Select an invertible r-column basis B by Gaussian elimination. Then det B=+-1. For A=B^{-1}M, Cramer's rule expresses every entry as a maximal minor of M divided by det B. Therefore every entry of A is0,+1,-1, including the basis identity columns. Both A and transformed RHS B^{-1}b are computed with polynomially many rational arithmetic operations; intermediate encoding lengths are polynomial in the original input length. The constraints Mx=b are exactly equivalent to Ax=B^{-1}b.

The coordinate-extremum LPs of823C can therefore use inequality matrix [A;-A;I;-I], a matrix with entries0,+1,-1. The theorem of Tardos1986 gives a polynomial number of arithmetic operations in the encoding size of this matrix, independent of the encodings of RHS and objective. Since that matrix's entries have constant size,6n LP solves, plus the normalization and comparisons in823C, give a strongly polynomial number of arithmetic operations in r,n, with all number sizes polynomial in total input encoding. Reading/writing arbitrarily long numbers still has their ordinary bit cost: this is not a bit-time bound independent of input length. Nonempty/bounded fibers and unimodularity are promises in this statement; no strong-polynomial recognition claim is needed. Exact equality remains independent of the positive external field w. This is theoretical: controls823 enumerate small fibers and do not implement Tardos or certify running-time behavior.
