# Correction829: integer-box promise is essential

The frozen823 algorithm paragraph says 'binary-encoded rational/integer input'. This is ambiguous and must not be used to extend the theorem to rational coordinate bounds. The corrected statement retains ALL assumptions of823B: M has integer entries and full row rank with nonzero maximal minors+-1, each b_s is integral, and both box endpoints l,u are integral (or extended bounds with bounded fibers). Rational arithmetic may be used internally by an exact LP solver. The strong-polynomial addendum827 inherits these same assumptions. No extension to arbitrary rational boxes is claimed.

The auditor824 supplied an exact falsifier for that extension. Let M=[1,1], use 0<=x<=1/2 and 0<=y<=2, and take b_-=0,b_0=1,b_+=2. The three integer-point enumerators are1,z_y,z_y^2. Thus F_0^2=F_-F_+ identically, including at every positive external field. However P_- is a point while P_0 and P_+ are segments. They cannot be translations of P_-. The coordinate-extrema algorithm applied to these real fibers would also reject equality: m_+=(0,3/2),m_0=(0,1/2), hence h=(0,1), whereas m_-=(0,0) differs from m_0-h=(0,-1/2). The LP extrema need not be integral. This falsifies the unqualified rational-box extension, not the integer-box theorem.

Frozen823 and827 remain unchanged; the objection and this correction belong to the audit trail, not an erased attempt. No score or manuscript assessment is created by this correction.

## A different, valid rational-box interface (not the rejected assertion)

For finite rational l,u, integer-point sets can first be replaced exactly by the integer box ceil(l)<=x<=floor(u). Apply the integer-box theorem and algorithm to THESE rounded fibers. Their polytopes, not the original real fibers, determine the translation criterion. If any rounded lower bound exceeds its upper bound, every integer fiber is empty. More generally, use feasibility LPs on the rounded integral box. If the central rounded fiber is empty, both endpoint fibers cannot be nonempty: averaging feasible endpoint points gives a feasible central point, and unimodular integrality then gives an integer central point. Thus the polynomial equality is0=0. If the center is nonempty but at least one endpoint is empty, the inequality is strict. If all three are nonempty, the integer-box translation criterion applies. This gives a polynomial bit-complexity equality algorithm for finite rational bounds after rounding, including empty integer fibers. It does NOT give a translation assertion about the original rational real fibers. No strongly polynomial arithmetic bound is asserted for the rational-rounding preprocessing under a model allowing only rational +,-,*,/,comparison operations; the827 strong bound remains stated for integer-box input.

In the auditor's example, rounding x<=1/2 to x<=0 turns each rounded fiber into the singleton(0,b). These rounded fibers ARE symmetric integral translations, correctly explaining the counting equality while retaining the original counterexample to translation of real rational fibers.
