MATHEMATICAL ASSESSMENT

1. Definitions and the target lemma (manuscript page 1).

The forward convention Pi_n = A_last(T^(n-1) omega)...A_first(omega) agrees
exactly with source equation (A.1), PDF page 34. Equations (A.2) and (A.3) there
define the same first and eventual site-count onsets. The source's S* is local
matrix adjunction on the same forward base. Its Lemma A.1 indeed asserts almost
sure equality of both lengths, and its proof asserts a word identity that leaves
the time arguments incorrectly interchanged. Reversing a label word alone cannot
repair this in a site-dependent process. The manuscript targets the actual v1
statement, not a weakened or misquoted substitute. The autonomous exception is
correct.

The manuscript's Choi convention is (id tensor Phi)(|Omega><Omega|), whereas the
source uses the swapped tensor orientation. These are consistent rank tests;
the manuscript states its orientation and its column-vectorized controls match
it. There is no normalization or Choi-orientation error in J(L)=I_4/2.

2. Two-phase bistochastic construction (Theorem 1, page 2).

Let U=Ad_H and D be Z dephasing. DUD=L, D^2=D and U^2=id hold exactly.
At starting phase 0, original block maps are D, UD, L; at starting phase 1
they are UD, L. Taking adjoints locally without changing the base gives D, DU:
the corresponding initial blocks are D,L and DU,DU,L. Each pre-depolarizing
block has Kraus-span dimension 2 and L has dimension 4. Every following local
map is unital, so Phi L=L. This proves all lengths, not merely a finite sample.
Both normalizations sum A_i* A_i=I and sum A_i A_i*=I hold at both phases.

The uniform two-cycle is invertible, measure preserving and ergodic; mixing is
not a hypothesis of the target appendix. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges
nonmixing. Consequently the pointwise discrepancy is a valid counterexample even
under bistochasticity, local faithfulness, dual faithfulness and finite eventual
injectivity. Its onset laws happen to coincide; this example alone would not
disprove equality in distribution. The manuscript correctly avoids that leap.

3. Three-phase construction (Theorem 2, pages 2-3).

For D,UD,D_X with D_X=UDU, the forward cumulative block maps are exactly
(D,UD,UD,L), (UD,UD,L), and (D_X,L), depending on starting phase. For the
forward pointwise-adjoint sequence D,DU,D_X, all three adjacent two-step
products are L. All local maps have rank-2 Choi matrices and are bistochastic.
Persistence of L therefore gives original first/eventual onsets (4,3,2) and
pointwise-adjoint first/eventual onsets (2,2,2) for every length. The CDFs at 2
are 1/3 and 1. This separately refutes a distributional repair on the unchanged
forward base. It is not merely a phase relabeling of Theorem 1.

4. Actual inverse-base identity (Proposition 3, page 3).

With hat T=T^-1 and hat A_i(omega)=A_i(T^-1 omega)*, expansion gives
hat Pi_n(omega;sigma)=Pi_n(T^-n omega;reverse(sigma))*.
The word-label reversal is bijective, and adjunction is conjugate-linear but
dimension preserving. Thus hat W_n(omega)=W_n(T^-n omega)*. Invariance of the
measure proves the fixed-n law without ergodicity or normalization assumptions.
The event relation hat E_n=T^n E_n has the correct sign. Because this shift
depends on n, equality of individual event probabilities alone does not identify
the unions defining first occurrence or the intersections/unions defining
eventual persistence. This warning is mathematically necessary.

The shift by one is the chosen endpoint convention, not an additional physical
principle: choosing A_i(omega)* on the inverse base would yield a differently
anchored identity with T^-(n-1). The stated convention and formula are correct.

5. Two-sided-faithfulness law theorem (Theorem 4, pages 3-4).

For positive maps in finite dimensions, faithfulness in the source's sense is
equivalent to Phi*(I)>0: Tr(Phi(p))=Tr(p Phi*(I)) for p>=0 proves both
directions. Thus the theorem's hypotheses give F=Phi(I)>0 and G=Phi*(I)>0.
The displayed decompositions Y=sum A_i(A_i* F^-1 Y) and
Y=sum (Y G^-1 A_i*)A_i are correct. The first says that an already full span
remains full after a later left multiplication by the local Kraus space. The
same argument for the reversed process uses original-map faithfulness. Taking
the intersection over all integer shifts supplies the required common invariant
full-measure set.

Both E_n and hat E_n are therefore increasing in n; first and eventual onsets
coincide in each process. The fixed-n identity then identifies their CDFs and
their atom at infinity. This does not imply equality at the same starting
phase. The genuine reverse of the three-cycle has onsets (4,2,3), as claimed.
No trace-preservation hypothesis is accidentally used in this proof.

The persistence ingredient is essentially source Lemma A.3 (page 35), applied
also to the correctly reversed process. The current proof supplies a useful
direct algebraic presentation, but this part should be explicitly attributed
rather than left to appear wholly new.

6. Reset obstruction (section 4, page 4).

R=(E_10,E_11) and B=(diag(1,1/2),sqrt(3)E_01/2) are trace preserving.
R(I)=2E_11 is singular; hence R* is not faithful. With V=span R and Q=span B,
the identities QV=M_2, VM_2=V and VQ=V are correct. They make the original
spans alternate: starting at 0, full exactly at even lengths; starting at 1,
full exactly at odd lengths >=3. Both eventual onsets are infinite.

For C=V* and Q*=span B*, CQ*=M_2, Q*M_2=M_2, Q*C=C and CC=C all hold.
Also CM_2=M_2, which follows directly or from C(CQ*)=(CC)Q*. Starting at 0,
the genuine reverse becomes full at length 2 and stays full; starting at 1 it
stays in C forever. Thus reversed first/eventual onsets are (2,infinity).
These finite-state subspace recurrences prove the infinite tails. The equal
fixed-block full-span probabilities 1/2 for every n>=2 coexist with unequal
first-onset laws and eventual-finiteness probabilities 0 versus 1/2. This is
a valid obstruction to dropping persistence, and it does not claim that the
two-sided-faithfulness condition is a necessary condition in every process.
