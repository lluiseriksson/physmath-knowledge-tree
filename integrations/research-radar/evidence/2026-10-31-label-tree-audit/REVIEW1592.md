## 1. Exact local-noise response

Work on the finite union of selected scalar coordinates. If C_F>=B^(-1)I and c=1/(2B), then C_F-cI>=cI. Hence independent centered Gaussians Y and Z with covariances C_F-cI and cI exist, and X=Y+Z has exactly covariance C_F. For different overlap components the coordinate unions U_j are disjoint. Consequently the Z restrictions are independent, even though the Y restrictions generally are not, and

    E G(Y+Z) = E_Y product_j E_Zj G_j(Y_Uj+Z_j).

No statement here sets a leaf-leaf entry of C_F-cI to zero. The factors G_j, their thresholds, their signs and their selected supports are unchanged. The convolution is an auxiliary representation of the same integral; it is not the substitution of a new smooth cutoff in the original law.

Write g_j(y)=integral G_j(x) phi_c(x-y) dx. Differentiating the kernel with respect to y gives

    partial_y^alpha phi_c(x-y)
      = c^(-|alpha|/2) He_alpha((x-y)/sqrt(c)) phi_c(x-y).

The sign is positive with this convention. This proves LOCAL-NOISE1591 lines 22–23 and never differentiates G_j. Bounded measurable factors are immediate. For the inherited growing interaction factors, the asserted smallness/growth condition must remain in force: a finite product is bounded by a finite polynomial times exp[t0 m ||x||^2] times a finite constant. With 0<=t0<=a/(32m), on an enlarged covariance band C'<=2a^(-1)I the Gaussian density has decay at least exp[-a||x||^2/4]. There is a strict positive margin for every finite polynomial derivative. A local neighborhood of C_F also keeps C'-cI positive. These facts justify the kernel differentiations, Gaussian integration by parts and differentiating under the residual integral. Merely calling an arbitrary convolution smooth, without an integrability neighborhood, would not justify the calculation; the target explicitly retains that requirement.

For one unordered component edge {i,j}, the affine symmetric direction H_ij has entries C_original(x,y) and their transpose for x in U_i, y in U_j. Therefore

    (1/2) H_ij : partial_y^2
      = sum_(x in U_i,y in U_j) C_original(x,y) partial_x partial_y.

The two symmetric entries cancel the heat-operator factor 1/2. The derivative coefficient is the ORIGINAL covariance entry, not its path-minimum-weighted value at the evaluation point. With c fixed, the same H differentiates C-cI. There is no derivative of c and no derivative of the nonsmooth path-minimum map. A finite forest formula evaluates these affine derivatives at forest covariances; it does not ask for a chain-rule differentiation of the parameter map.

Applying k distinct edge operators gives one endpoint choice per edge, 2k scalar field derivatives, and c^(-k). The operators commute and each independent edge variable is differentiated once, so no k! or multiindex factorial is added. ## 2. Original-label injection and factorial payment

Fix an original selected set S of s distinct labels. Its support-overlap components S_j and their disjoint coordinate unions U_j are determined by S. Choose one internal spanning tree in each overlap component and, for each scalar coordinate in that component, one label containing it as owner. Both choices are deterministic functions of S, fixed before selecting a component tree or any external endpoints. The choices need not be compatible across different S; the argument is performed separately for each set.

For a component tree T and an endpoint assignment, join the two owner labels of each external covariance edge and add the chosen internal trees. If there are N overlap components, there are s-N internal edges and N-1 external edges. The resulting graph is connected and has s-1 edges; contraction of the internal trees gives T, so it is an ordinary tree on S. No parallel external pair can be created by different component edges: a pair of owner labels determines its pair of components, and T has only one edge between any such pair.

Retaining internal/external colors and the external scalar endpoints makes the map injective. Deleting the external edges recovers the internal component trees; contraction recovers T; the endpoint decorations recover each assignment. Internal trees are not being summed freely. Enlarging the image later to all colored trees is an inequality with nonnegative weights, so the fact that most colored trees are inadmissible costs no missing multiplicity.

Let e_i be the number of external endpoints owned by label i. Because each coordinate x has one owner, e_i=sum_(owner(x)=i) alpha_x. The multinomial coefficient gives

    product_(owner(x)=i) alpha_x! <= e_i!,

and e_i<=d_i yields the target product_x alpha_x! <= product_i e_i! <= product_i d_i!. This argument would fail if the same scalar coordinate could change owner from one edge to another: two occurrences split between two owners would incorrectly compare 2! with 1!1!. The target rules out precisely that defect.

The internal edges have weight one when their labels overlap. After the degree factorial has been bounded on original labels, summing scalar endpoints on each external edge is bounded by c^(-1) sum_(x in D_i,y in D_j)|C_xy|. Releasing the owner restrictions only adds nonnegative terms. Summing the two color choices edge by edge gives the product of

    w(i,j)=1_(i!=j and D_i intersects D_j)
             +2B sum_(x in D_i,y in D_j)|C_xy|.

This explains the domination in LABEL-TREE1592 lines 44–45 without silently assigning another component or orientation factorial. For a selected set with one overlap component there is no external derivative; |E G|<=||G||_2<=lambda^s. Its canonical internal tree alone has every internal edge weight at least one and degree factorials at least one, so the same tree majorant covers it. For s=1, the separate bound |z({i})|<=lambda is correct. For N>=2 each tree parameter domain is [0,1]^(N-1), of volume one; integrating a uniform bound adds no factor.

## 3. Row summation and original rooted-set enumeration

The multiplicity bound is on ORIGINAL scalar coordinates and on the ENTIRE actual label universe. Therefore

    sum_j sum_(x in D_i,y in D_j)|C_xy|
      = sum_(x in D_i) sum_y multiplicity(y)|C_xy|
      <= D m J.

The internal overlap term contributes at most Delta. This proves sum_j w(i,j)<=Delta+2BDmJ<=R. Symmetry follows from the real covariance symmetry. When label repetitions are later allowed, the diagonal w(i,i) may be positive because of the covariance term; it is included in this same row sum. The internal-overlap part alone excludes i=j. No diagonal contribution has been lost.

There is no additional sum over translated overlap components after this step. All original labels, attachment coordinates and their covariance entries have already been included in the row sum. Multiplying by another set-distance placement factor would double-charge a different majorant and could spoil this proof. Conversely, the n^2 translated-component witness from REVIEW1575 does not contradict this argument: it disproves a size-independent bound for a merged component attachment, not the bounded row sum of each original bounded-support label.

Fix r and s>=2. The correct identity for the nonnegative tree majorant M(S) is

    sum_(S contains r, |S|=s) M(S)
      = 1/(s-1)! sum_(i_2,...,i_s distinct, all different from r)
                    sum_(trees tau on [s])
                    product_v sqrt(deg_tau(v)!)
                    product_(ab in tau) w(i_a,i_b),  i_1=r.

Every original set has exactly (s-1)! orderings, and the COMPLETE abstract tree sum is invariant under permuting the nonroot positions. An individual tree need not be invariant; invariance is used only after summing all trees. The canonical owner/internal choices do not need to be permutation-equivariant because they have already been bounded by that symmetric full majorant. The formula concerns s original labels, not N overlap components and not a polymer-family Mayer coefficient.

Drop distinctness in the nonnegative expression. For a fixed abstract tree, orient toward vertex 1 and successively sum leaves other than the root. Each sum is at most R. The remaining degree factorials depend only on the abstract tree, so they stay outside the placement sum. This gives R^(s-1) even when different abstract vertices receive the same actual label. Thus no hidden root factor, original label placement count, or component N! remains.

For degrees d_i=l_i+1, with sum_i l_i=s-2, the number of numbered trees is (s-2)!/product_i l_i!. Hence exactly

    sum_tau product_i sqrt(d_i!)
      = (s-2)! [z^(s-2)] F(z)^s,
    F(z)=sum_(l>=0) sqrt((l+1)!)/l! z^l.

Rewrite the coefficient at z=1 as sqrt((l+1)/l!). Cauchy–Schwarz with factors sqrt((l+1)2^l/l!) and 2^(-l/2) yields

    F(1)^2 <= (sum_l (l+1)2^l/l!)(sum_l 2^(-l))
             = (3e^2)(2)=6e^2.

All coefficients are nonnegative, so [z^(s-2)]F(z)^s<=F(1)^s<=A^s with A=e sqrt(6). Dividing (s-2)! by (s-1)! leaves exactly 1/(s-1). The resulting bound is

    sum_(S contains r, |S|=s)|z(S)|
      <= lambda^s R^(s-1) A^s/(s-1),  s>=2.

This includes s=2; it is loose there but valid. Let x=lambda A R. For 0<=x<1,

    sum_(s>=2) lambda^s R^(s-1) A^s/(s-1)
      = lambda A sum_(n>=1) x^n/n
      = -lambda A log(1-x).

Adding the singleton gives exactly lambda-lambda A log(1-lambda A R). For exp(eta|S|), put lambda_eta=lambda exp(eta) in every occurrence and require lambda_eta A R<1. There is no claimed useful uniform bound at equality or beyond it. Failure of this sufficient condition does not prove divergence of the actual finite or infinite activity sum.
