PROOF AND SCOPE AUDIT

1. Model, physical normalization, and the classical limit (source lines 54-180; PDF pp. 1-3).

The law is fixed across a block, with independent fading draws conditional on that law. The decoder is common to the compound family. The photon constraint is a block-average constraint and the error criterion is uniformly vanishing average error. These are necessary qualifiers, not cosmetic choices. Compactness comes from the compact parameter rectangle and weak closedness of the nonempty family. Nonemptiness already entails K >= 0, although writing this explicitly would improve the setup.

The measure-and-prepare identity has the right heterodyne convention and noise subtraction. Heterodyne adds one unit per mode, and the prepared thermal variance subtracts k=|a|^2. The E >= 2K/v_* regime gives the uniform lower noise floor used later. The identity extends with an arbitrary reference; it is not merely an equality on coherent states. The induced Born decoding kernel is common to every law. On the direct side coherent preparation followed by heterodyne gives the plus-noise classical channel without an encoder side-information assumption. Thus the exact simulation sandwich is legitimate for arbitrary block encodings and common joint measurements, including entangled inputs.

The leading quantity is sup_P inf_nu I(P;W_nu), with E-dependent physical energy removed by normalization. The O(1/E) bounds are absolute rate bounds in the declared simultaneous signal-and-noise scaling. They are not uniform statements over different scalings, growing parameter bounds, or arbitrary low-noise channels.

2. Entropy continuity and noise sensitivity (lines 184-261; pp. 3-4).

The bounded-density plus uniformly bounded second-moment lemma is adequate for the uses made of it. In its tail argument, normalization of the tail mass produces the second logarithm of that mass; the displayed upper bound has the correct sign and power. The moment bound makes tail entropy uniformly negligible. A pointwise density lower bound is not silently assumed.

With the paper's complex Gaussian convention, the heat equation has coefficient 1/4. The score and flux estimates give the stated 2D/ell bound for mutual-information variation under bounded variance changes. Truncating heavy-tailed inputs before differentiation and passing back by entropy continuity avoids an unjustified direct differentiation under only a second moment. The branch-revealed Gaussian upper bound makes all classical mutual informations finite, and concavity plus V(0)=0 justifies the rescaling V(1+u) <= (1+u)V(1). These constants support both sides of Theorem 1.

3. The continuous-alphabet compound coding theorem (lines 263-377; pp. 4-5).

The converse uses Fano, the exact block relative-entropy identity, and concavity at the common averaged input. In particular it does not replace sup-inf by inf-sup. The direct proof first creates strict cost slack, then truncates/quantizes inputs and quantizes outputs. Gaussian smoothing supplies the needed uniform density and continuity estimates. Nested output partitions and compactness justify the Dini step for this family.

For the resulting finite alphabets, constant-composition coding with a common maximum-mutual-information decoder supplies an exponentially small error estimate. The polynomial-size channel net and the expectation of the SUM of its errors produce one deterministic code good on that net. The n-times-total-variation estimate then extends it to the whole family. This is not the invalid interchange of expectation and supremum sometimes made in such arguments. Cost is controlled by word composition and the earlier slack. The approximation order preserves a common decoder and does not give either terminal knowledge of the unknown law. I found no missing uniform-blocklength assertion; the proof claims the limits it actually establishes.

4. Matched reset, strict improvement, and the entire Gaussian scheduling hull (lines 379-509; pp. 5-7).

For the matched-reset channel, the standard circular Gaussian input gives the matched Gaussian output. The flag conditional-entropy function H(x) is bounded, radial, nonaffine in the cost, and vanishes at large radius while remaining positive at finite radius. This permits a bounded compactly supported radial perturbation annihilating mass and cost but giving the desired sign for integral hH. Since output mass and second moment remain fixed, the change in output entropy is exactly controlled by relative entropy from the original Gaussian output. Its quadratic upper bound leaves a positive first-order gain. The perturbation therefore proves a strict non-Gaussian improvement without an unsupported numerical optimization.

The subsequent argument is stronger than showing that one Gaussian input is suboptimal. Injectivity yields strict concavity; rotational symmetry forces any hypothetical Gaussian optimizer to be circular and centered. Strict capacity growth follows from concavity and the unbounded lower bound obtained by revealing the reset flag. The Gaussian scheduling hull is attained: energy sublevels are compact in the Gaussian parameterization, and the sublinear logarithmic information bound controls the tails of a scheduling measure. The exact Jensen relative-entropy gap, together with injectivity, rules out an attaining Gaussian mixture at the unrestricted optimum. These points close the time-sharing loophole, including displaced, anisotropic, and singular planar Gaussians.

The coherent Gaussian codebook upper bound allows correlated Gaussian modulation and uses the physical coherent-state energy directly, without importing the extra heterodyne unit from the converse simulation. Its scope is the specified modulation class; the text correctly does not call this a separation from every possible Gaussian protocol.

5. Gaussian-average and marginal restrictions (lines 511-765; pp. 7-10).

The Gaussian-average Holevo bound covers arbitrary conditional signal states when the block average is globally Gaussian. The simulation and data processing reduce it to the classical input-output information, and tensorization only requires its Gaussian one-mode Husimi marginals. Continuous ensemble labels are treated through relative entropy rather than an undefined difference of possibly infinite quantities. The one-unit cost correction, the uniform noise perturbation, and concavity of H_G give the stated upper bound. Finite rational schedules of Gaussian coherent ensembles give the matching lower limit for the defined regularized functional. The manuscript does not incorrectly identify that functional with an operational capacity.

The trace-distance argument also survives scrutiny. The classical continuity lemma uses a common submeasure and a mixture flag. If total variation is strictly below the supplied d, shrinking the common submeasure gives an exact mixture parameter d; this matters because the correction is not being assumed monotone on its entire range. One binary entropy suffices after the two mixture identities are subtracted. Exceptional-component energy is at most P/d. Revealing the channel branch bounds mutual information even in situations where a mixed output differential entropy by itself would be a poor starting point.

For a moment-matched Gaussian reference, heterodyne contracts trace distance into classical total variation, and the reference has the same marginal energy. The minus-channel floor r/2 is available. The perspective form of the energy correction supports Jensen averaging despite highly unequal coordinate energies. Consequently the displayed robust Holevo bound requires only average one-mode trace-distance control plus the explicitly separate moment matching; it does not assume a globally Gaussian state or independent average-state marginals. The high-energy functional limit follows in the stated order of limits.

6. Typical independent Gaussian books and energy repair (lines 767-926; pp. 11-12).

The proof fixes E and a finite Gaussian schedule before the blocklength limit. A finite photon cutoff bounds the empirical trace error, with a tail controlled by the energy and a finite-rank sampling term. Choosing the cutoff proportional to sqrt(M) gives the advertised O(M^-1/4) expectation bound; Markov, the union over m coordinates, and exponential M make the failure probabilities summable. The Borel-Cantelli conclusion does not require independence between different blocklengths.

Moments are handled separately with Gaussian fourth moments. This separation is essential: trace convergence with an energy bound alone would not give convergence to the moment-matched Gaussian. The stated vacuum/rare-Fock counterexample correctly illustrates that failure. Continuity of the positive-semidefinite square root also handles singular covariance limits in the empirical Gaussian matching.

For saturated rather than slack schedules, the common amplitude rescaling has the right sign and restores the actual photon budget. The whole-block trace change is bounded by |1-t| sqrt(m E P_hat), which vanishes under the exponential sample-size estimates. Reliability transfers from a previously reliable book; it is not asserted merely from typicality. The corollary then uses Fano at fixed E before taking E large. The qualification about an uncorrected saturated Gaussian book's probability of feasibility is justified by the central limit theorem. No random-book decoder-existence claim is hidden in this section.

7. Entanglement assistance and adaptive backward feedback (lines 929-1081; pp. 13-14).

The model allows arbitrary finite preshared entanglement in each block, with dimension growing arbitrarily with blocklength. It does not bound assistance by a uniform rate, and it does not provide a free forward classical side channel. The initial resource is independent of message and law. The expected photon budget is imposed separately at EVERY law, which is the correct resource statement for the adaptive converse here.

Lemma 11's left side is a change in quantum mutual information, not optimized accessible information. The complete simulation retains the operator-valued conditional MR state after the heterodyne outcome X. The output Y is then generated through the classical channel, so data processing gives I(MR;Y) <= I(X;Y). The chain rule and nonnegativity of I(R;Y) give I(M;RY)-I(M;R) <= I(X;Y). The finite-energy branch-revealed bound establishes finiteness before this manipulation, while I(M;R) is bounded by H(M). Thus the proof does not subtract infinite von Neumann entropies or smuggle in a fully measured receiver reference.

Without feedback, one law-independent encoded block and one law-independent reference lead to the common-input sup-inf upper bound. With feedback, fix a law first and follow the actual receiver memory, including transcripts and retained instrument outputs. Sender-local operations cannot alter the receiver's message-reference state. Each forward channel use increases its quantum message information by at most the corresponding scalar-channel information. Receiver-local processing cannot increase that quantity. Telescoping and concavity use the unconditional expected input costs for that law and then its block budget; no per-history or almost-sure energy bound is inferred. Taking the infimum over laws only after this argument gives the stated inf-sup bound. I found no dimensional factor or entanglement-rate term left uncontrolled.

The finite-family achievability groups identical kernels and separates every distinct pair using finitely many finite-cost input/bin experiments. Training length tends to infinity while being negligible compared with blocklength. The payload is chosen from known-law codes with a common message size and strict energy slack. Every possible selected book obeys that slack, including the wrong selection, so the expected budget is not conditioned on successful identification. The fixed finite pilot cost is absorbed by slack. These statements are made at fixed E before limits remove the slack. The proof supplies no infinite-family feedback direct theorem, and the manuscript explicitly refrains from claiming one.

8. Strict learning/resource gap (lines 1082-1138; pp. 14-15).

The AWGN gain b is chosen so that its constrained capacity equals the reset channel's unrestricted capacity C1. The branch-revealed bounds put b^2 in (0,k). The AWGN optimizer is uniquely the circular unit Gaussian, whereas the reset channel strictly improves on that input. Compactness of the second-moment input set, smoothed output entropy continuity, and continuity of conditional entropy show that the common-input minimum actually attains its supremum. Hence equality with C1 is impossible; the gap is strictly positive rather than only an optimizer mismatch on a noncompact domain.

Vacuum pilots require no photon expenditure and discriminate the two plus-noise laws. The displayed bin probabilities differ strictly; the midpoint decision has the quoted Hoeffding exponent. At fixed E, ceil(a log m) pilots suffice for vanishing identification error for any a>0, and one backward bit selects the payload book. Combining the two finite-E approximation bounds gives a strict gap for all sufficiently large E. The conclusion compares no-feedback assistance and backward-feedback learning under the stated resources; it is not an entanglement-versus-classical communication slogan and it is not a strong converse.

The limitation is quantitative: C1, the gap Delta, and the threshold for E are not evaluated. The example is a theorem of existence with a channel parameter defined implicitly through a capacity. It is still valid, but its practical import cannot be inferred from strict positivity alone.

ANTECEDENTS, NOVELTY, AND SIGNIFICANCE

The exact downloaded versions, hashes, PDF page ranges read, retrieval dates, and limits of each comparison are in source_pins.json. Directed reading of those primary sources is not represented as a complete review of every cited paper. No foreign paper corpus is included in this assessment archive.

Berta, Gharibyan and Walter, arXiv:1603.02282v1, already give finite-dimensional entanglement-assisted compound formulas and finite-family feedback training leading to an inf-sup result. Boche, Janssen and Kaltenstadler, arXiv:1606.09314v3, provide the compound entanglement-assisted sup-inf formula, explicitly discuss assistance growth, and exhibit a finite classical/entanglement-breaking example with disjoint optimizing sets and a strict minimax gap. Their PDF calls the result Theorem 1; the corresponding HTML labels it Theorem 3.1. The manuscript's 3.1 reference is therefore not a false citation, though giving both locators would help. Shrader and Permuter, arXiv:0711.0705v1, contain classical compound-feedback learning and minimax results. These works mean that neither training to choose a law-dependent payload nor existence of a strict compound resource gap is a new general principle here. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.02282v1 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.09314v3 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.0705v1 .

Holevo's constrained entanglement-assisted capacity theorem in quant-ph/0211170v2 supplies important infinite-dimensional context, subject to its entropy/constraint hypotheses. It cannot simply be substituted for this paper's compound adaptive statement. Holevo's 0802.0235v1 gives the measure-and-prepare foundation for entanglement-breaking Gaussian channels. Holevo and Giovannetti's 1202.6480v1 explains the related Gaussian high-noise/classical limit. Thus the simulation mechanism and the single-channel intuition are established tools. The paper's contribution is the explicit law-uniform error control and its consistent integration with the specified compound and constrained settings. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0211170v2 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.0235v1 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.6480v1 .

Shi, Zhang and Zhuang, arXiv:1909.11112v4, give exact Gaussian entanglement-assisted benchmark expressions and discuss high-noise benefits. Their fixed-signal high-noise relative-benefit expression cannot be substituted uniformly into the present simultaneous signal/noise scaling. Inspecting the exact formulas is consistent with the present leading classical value; the comparison is a scaling inference from those formulas, not a statement that their entire receiver analysis has been re-proved. Hao et al., arXiv:2101.07482v1, demonstrate an entanglement-assisted benefit in a different weak-signal finite-energy regime. There is no contradiction with an O(1/E) absolute benefit in the present regime. The manuscript is right to avoid a general assertion that bright noise always erases assistance. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11112v4 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07482v1 .

Winter, arXiv:1507.07775v6, provides established common-part, mixture-entropy, and inflated-energy continuity methods. Shirokov, arXiv:1610.08870v5, supplies related energy-constrained continuity for ensembles and channels. The current average-marginal trace-distance restriction is a different distance statement; it must be proved, and the manuscript does prove it using additional moment matching. The abstract method is not new, but this application with an explicit per-mode bound and unequal energy allocation is useful. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.07775v6 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.08870v5 .

The universal finite-alphabet and approximation machinery has classical and classical-quantum antecedents. Bjelakovic and Boche, arXiv:0710.3027v2, state the classical compound formula and its error-criterion distinctions. Schaefer and Loyka, arXiv:1509.02182v1, use channel nets and uniform approximation in a related setting. The author-hosted December 20, 2005 draft by Loyka, Charalambous and Mitran gives compactness/Dini quantization arguments, but its receiver-side channel knowledge must not be imported into the current unknown-law decoder. The paper supplies its own common-decoder argument, so that mismatch is not a gap. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.3027v2 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.02182v1 ; https://ece.uwaterloo.ca/~pmitran/compound3.pdf .

Bosonic fading/non-Gaussian antecedents also limit broad novelty claims. Huber and Koenig, arXiv:1710.06734v2, bound the Gaussian-coding loss for certain non-Gaussian bosonic channels; that is not the same uniformly scaled random-gain family or a strict hull separation. Tu et al.'s 2026 real fading analysis treats a different real equal-noise model and provides relevant strict-concavity/injectivity and non-Gaussian-optimization context. I do not rely on its full finite-support theorem, whose proof dependencies were not audited. Catalano et al., arXiv:2604.26874v2, treat entanglement-assisted fading with non-Gaussian optimality and closely related symmetry/injectivity arguments. These are meaningful antecedents, not interchangeable theorems. Adesso, Lami and Giovannetti, arXiv:2610.22308v1, concern Gaussian-channel optimality; the reset mixture here is not a single Gaussian channel. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.06734v2 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s44459-026-00074-7.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26874v2 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22308v1 .

Within that comparison, I did not locate an exact antecedent to the complete uniform compound bound plus attained strict Gaussian scheduling hull, marginal robustness, typical-book consequence, and constrained bosonic learning construction as proved here. This is a bounded literature finding, not an exhaustive priority certificate. Section 7 is best regarded as a mathematically useful resource-accounting extension and explicit bosonic realization of known minimax/training ideas. The retained Gaussian-hull and marginal results carry more of the manuscript's distinct technical contribution than the general fact that feedback can learn a finite compound law.

MATERIAL OBJECTIONS AND REVISIONS

No material mathematical objection was identified, and there are no required theorem or proof repairs in this assessment. The following limitations affect the importance judgment and must remain explicit in any presentation of the work:

- The scaling fixes bounded gain and a strictly positive normalized-noise floor while both physical signal and noise grow. Conclusions outside it are unsupported.
- The strict example is an implicit constructed channel pair with no computed capacity gap or finite-energy threshold. Practical advantage and useful rates cannot be claimed from this proof alone.
- Finite-family training, compound minimax order, Gaussian measure-and-prepare simulation, and entropy continuity methods are antecedent tools. The manuscript's careful priority language is necessary.
- The typical-book result is an upper restriction on the specified independently sampled books and a reliability-transfer statement after repair, not an independent achievability theorem for arbitrary Gaussian encodings.

Minor clarifications recommended before release: explicitly write 0 <= K < infinity; replace the phrase 'the lower channel' near the sandwich by 'the channel used for the lower bound' or the W-plus symbol to avoid confusing lower rate with lower noise; give both PDF/HTML theorem locators for Boche et al. None is a mathematical blocker. The rendered PDF has no observed clipped equations, missing pages, or material layout defect. A numerical lower bound on the constructed gap and a more physically motivated fading example would increase usefulness, but they are suggestions for development, not invented prerequisites for the stated existence theorem.
