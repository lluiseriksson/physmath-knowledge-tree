## Fixed finite carriers

Use the actual one-step raw FREE coordinates and the per-coarse-bond selected reconstruction in1546. This reconstruction was conditionally supported by assessment1547; the full historical law/geometry dictionary remains open. The local inverse solves the corresponding finite group constraint; it is not the nonlocal multiscale response H_k for k>0.

Wilson plaquettes involve their four bond endpoints. After substitution, their carriers lie in the union of those endpoint blocks, within the radius-one coarse l-infinity neighborhood of an anchor block. A gauge contour factor is intrablock. A Haar logarithm for a selected output and the determinant of its selected constraint have the two endpoint-block carrier. Unselected Haar factors have one-coordinate carriers. These claims use the exact per-c block-diagonal selection1546, and would fail for a different constraint convention coupling different selected coarse bonds.

For fixed odd L, dimension d and Lie algebra dimension r, one conservative scalar-coordinate bound is

    d0=3^d d (L+2)^d r.

There are at most

    p=[d(d-1)/2+d+1] L^d+d

anchored factors per coarse block: positively oriented Wilson plaquettes, Haar bonds, soft gauge contours and selected constraints, respectively. This is an upper bound; some terms can be absent. With the above radius-one carrier convention each scalar coordinate is in at most m=3^d p factors. Large harmless overcounts are intentional. For a small torus, use periodic carriers and unique coordinates; wraparound can decrease these counts. No new growing R_j collar is inserted into these bare finite carriers.

## The pointwise inequality lives on the original cutoff

The bare t0 quadratic-plus-local-constant estimate1539 uses |g0 A|<=K delta0 on the original FREE cutoff. It is not a global Taylor bound on every Gaussian configuration. Let K be that original product cutoff. For each factor choose a measurable local set K_i containing every restriction A|D_i with A in K on which its chart and the bound |v_i|<=t0 W_i hold. Define f_i=exp(v_i)-1 on K_i and f_i=0 elsewhere. Then exactly on K,

    exp(sum_i v_i)=product_i(1+f_i),

and every coefficient of the finite expansion of the UNNORMALIZED integral is E[1_K product_(i in S) f_i]. It satisfies the1563 bound (Kconst t0)^|S| by domination, since the local zero extension satisfies the required bound globally. Kconst denotes the lemma constant, not the cutoff set.

This zero extension is only a real integration bound. It is not a holomorphic extension in A, background fields, or interpolation tags. It cannot be differentiated through its artificial boundary to obtain analytic contour estimates. The exact cutoff indicator remains in every coefficient; removing it to factorize integrals changes the law. Normalizing by its probability still requires that cost, as recorded1559/1563.
