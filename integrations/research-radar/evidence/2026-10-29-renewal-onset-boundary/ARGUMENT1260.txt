Stationary resets: qualitative injectivity does not control the mean onset

Status: own analytic derivation and exact rational controls; not independently reviewed, not a Lean theorem, not a new manuscript or a novelty claim. This is a standard renewal mechanism applied to the already identified quantitative boundary. No assessment of published v2 transfers to this extension, and the published PDF is unchanged.

Set p_k=4/[k(k+1)(k+2)] on positive integers. The identity p_k=2/[k(k+1)]-2/[(k+1)(k+2)] gives total mass1 and tail P(G>=r)=2/[r(r+1)]. Also k p_k=4[1/(k+1)-1/(k+2)], so E G=2. Thus this is a genuine finite-mean renewal gap law, with infinite second moment.

Consider the countable countdown chain: from r>=2 go deterministically to r-1; from1 choose the next state G with law p. All states communicate through1, and p_1=2/3 supplies a self-loop. The return time to1 when starting at1 is G, including the departure step, so its mean is2. The chain is irreducible, aperiodic and positive recurrent. Its invariant probability pi_r=1/[r(r+1)] is normalized by telescoping and satisfies pi_r=pi_(r+1)+pi_1 p_r exactly. This direct balance check avoids any ambiguous extracted indexing in reference notes.

Use the bilateral stationary Markov law on positive-integer paths, whose finite-dimensional distributions are pi at the left endpoint times the relevant transition probabilities. These distributions are consistent by stationarity, defining the two-sided process. The left shift T is invertible and probability preserving. The standard countable-chain convergence theorem applies: the displayed irreducibility, positive recurrence and aperiodicity give P^n(i,j)->pi_j. It yields mixing first for finite cylinder events via the Markov property and bounded convergence, then for all measurable events by cylinder approximation. Hence the stationary shift is ergodic. The state1 visit frequency is pi_1=1/2 by the state-occupation theorem. No independence between successive local channels is asserted.

Let the qubit channel at time t be the identity when R_t>1 and the complete depolarizing reset L(a)=Tr(a)I2/2 when R_t=1. Both channels are CPTP, unital and self-adjoint in the Hilbert-Schmidt pairing; local and dual faithfulness hold uniformly. The unnormalized output-first Choi matrices are J(Id)=vec(I2)vec(I2)^* (rank1) and J(L)=I4/2 (rank4). The exact relations L^2=L and L Id=Id L=L show that a product is identity before its first reset and exactly L afterward, irrespective of subsequent reset choices.

Use the manuscript's length convention: a block of length n includes times0 through n-1. If R_0=r, the first reset is at time r-1. Consequently the first full-Choi and eventual full-Choi onsets agree and equal N=r, including the r=1 case. Under stationary pi, P(N>n)=1/(n+1) for every integer n>=0. Thus N<infinity almost surely, but E N=sum_(n>=0)1/(n+1)=infinity. This is not the mean renewal return time: sampling at a stationary arbitrary time weights long gaps differently from starting at a reset.

The limiting target is the constant faithful state I2/2, with deterministic smallest eigenvalue1/2. Products equal its replacement channel exactly after N. For any fixed q in(0,1), the induced trace norm on all complex matrices obeys
 ||Phi^(n)-L||_(1->1) <= 2 q^(-N) q^n.
Indeed both CPTP maps have induced trace norm1, so the left side is at most2 before N and zero after. The prefactor is finite almost surely. Its expectation is infinite because pi_r q^(-r) does not tend to zero. An almost-sure exponential estimate with an uncontrolled random prefactor therefore does not imply an integrable onset. This any-q statement is special to the reset construction, not a revised reading of the imported convergence theorem's existential rate.

Hypothesis eliminated: one cannot infer finite expected injectivity onset merely from the published qualitative assumptions, even adding constant faithful target, bistochastic local maps, fixed positive reset frequency, an invertible mixing base and eventual exact convergence. A moment or quantitative dependence/tail assumption is still necessary for such an inference. This does not refute the qualitative injectivity theorem or supply a new general mixing theorem. A possible quantitative bound would need explicit integrability/tail information on the prefactor or waiting times; no sharp sufficient theorem is proved here.

Falsifiers: failure of gap normalization, invariant balance, bilateral stationarity, onset indexing, Choi ranks or all-length composition invalidates the construction. These are addressed by the displayed analytic arguments; the finite program is corroboration rather than proof by extrapolation. Any future claim of integrable onset from weaker hypotheses must be tested against this example. Abandon a standalone-paper route if the only contribution is this textbook inspection-paradox specialization.
