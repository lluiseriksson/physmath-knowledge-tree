979: A harmonic-weight bound for random-unitary comparisons
Parent derivation,27 October2026. Unreviewed; no manuscript, score or priority claim.
Not part of the already published exact957 manuscript or its assessment958.

Let U_1,...,U_m be unitaries on A, m>=2, E=(1/m)sum_j Ad(U_j),
and N_w=sum_j w_j Ad(U_j), all w_j>0 and sum w_j=1.
Extend both maps by identity on arbitrary finite R. Consider pairs rho,sigma
with E_R(rho)=E_R(sigma) and finite positive D(rho||sigma).
The proposed bound is
 D(N_w,R(rho)||N_w,R(sigma)) <= h(w) D(rho||sigma),
 h(w)=1-m^2/(sum_j 1/w_j).
It is nonnegative by Cauchy--Schwarz and strictly below1 for positive weights.
This does not assert the exact coefficient, independence of every input
constraint, or any novelty relative to the full contraction literature.

Self-contained metric proof. For positive W define the BKM quadratic form
g_W(Z)=integral_0^1 <Z,[(1-s)L_W+sR_W]^(-1)Z> ds.
For positive Q, <Z,Q^-1 Z>=sup_Y(2Re<Y,Z>-<Y,QY>), so this
inverse quadratic form is jointly convex in Q,Z. Thus g is jointly convex
in W,Z. It is quadratic in arbitrary Hermitian tangent Z (including negative
scalar multiples), and invariant under simultaneous unitary conjugation.
These are established metric properties, not claimed new machinery.

For a faithful base W and Hermitian Z with E_R(Z)=0, put
W_j=U_j W U_j*, Z_j=U_j Z U_j*. Then sum_j Z_j=0.
For ANY real t,
 N_w(Z)=sum_j(w_j-t)Z_j=sum_j w_j[(w_j-t)/w_j]Z_j.
Joint convexity and unitary invariance imply
 g_(N_w W)(N_w Z) <= sum_j (w_j-t)^2/w_j *g_W(Z).
The scalar sum equals1-2mt+t^2 sum_j1/w_j. Minimize at
t=m/(sum_j1/w_j) to get h(w). Negative tangent coefficients are allowed;
one must NOT apply joint convexity of state relative entropy directly to
negative state weights. The convex weights here are always the positive w_j.

The entire affine segment W_s=sigma+s(rho-sigma) has E_R(rho-sigma)=0.
Apply the metric bound pointwise and integrate with weight1-s to obtain
the finite-pair relative-entropy bound. For nonfaithful states, mix both
with the same maximally mixed state. This preserves E equality. Joint
convexity plus lower semicontinuity gives convergence of finite divergences
on input and output; output support inclusion follows from positivity.
Hence pass the inequality before division. No uniform continuity in dim R
is assumed. All unitaries act locally, but the argument is valid for each
finite R with the SAME constant.

Full local depolarization: use a d-dimensional unitary error basis, m=d^2,
U_1=I, with E_R(X)=I_A/d tensor Tr_A X. Set
w_1=[1+(m-1)lambda]/m and w_j=(1-lambda)/m for j>1,0<lambda<1.
Then N=lambda id+(1-lambda)E and elementary algebra gives
h(w)=(m-1)lambda^2/[1+(m-2)lambda].
E_R(rho)=E_R(sigma) is precisely equal R marginals. Thus this supplies an
upper bound for the unrestricted complete pair coefficient, and hence an
upper bound for actual-product MI. It does NOT prove proposed971 equality.

For qubits lambda1/2: proposed bound3/8 versus the earlier order-only2/5,
with the audited SEP MI lower1/3. The remaining interval is[1/3,3/8].
In general the order-only bound m lambda^2/[1+(m-1)lambda] minus this bound
is exactly lambda^2(1-lambda)/[(1+(m-1)lambda)(1+(m-2)lambda)]>0.
This comparison concerns the earlier order bound, not a claim to the best
published bound. A covering literature theorem could make979 only a
rederivation even though the algebra is correct.

Scope limits and next checks: one error-basis mixture, no exactness, no
conditional coefficient subtracting unequal reference divergences, no
capacity theorem or tensor-product-channel conclusion. Scientific priority
has not been established. Check the proof and its closest general quantum
Fisher/random-unitary predecessors before any new manuscript or assessment.
Falsifier: a valid equal-E pair above h(w), or a failure of the metric
