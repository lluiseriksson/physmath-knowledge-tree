## Same first cutoff: large in Gaussian units, small physically

For the first law take the ACTUAL free Gaussian-coordinate threshold r_chi=c_chi delta0/g0, with a fixed positive source/norm conversion c_chi. Use c_chi=1 only in matched source coordinates. Write ell=log(g0^-2), delta0=C_delta exp(-ell/2) ell^p, C_delta>0 fixed. Then

    r_chi^2=c_chi^2 C_delta^2 ell^(2p) -> infinity,
    delta0 -> 0.

Thus the tail can become small while the physical analytic radius also becomes small. This is not contradictory. It is also different from1535: the fixed ratio delta0/epsilon0=C_delta/A0 does NOT improve with ell.

Let a=(gamma_2/20)c_chi^2. For a target D delta0, the exact logarithmic tail inequality is

    a C_delta^2 ell^(2p)
        >= ell/2 -p log ell -log(D C_delta).

For ell>=1 and p>1/2, a conservative sufficient pair is

    ell^(2p-1) >= 1/(a C_delta^2),
    ell >= 2 max(0,-log(D C_delta)).

The left side then exceeds ell, paying ell/2 and the possible constant deficit, with -p log ell only helping. Strict inequalities give a strict margin. In addition choose a C_delta^2 ell^(2p) larger than the fixed geometric/decay thresholds of2.31–2.32. The exponential correction 4M^4 exp[-2a C_delta^2 ell^(2p)] vanishes. All finite interaction-smallness conditions proportional to delta0 are simultaneously met by increasing ell, with D,C3 and geometric constants held fixed. The source choice p>5r with r>=2 is safely above1/2.

For this INSPECTED Gaussian tail gate there is therefore no incompatibility between a positive fixed C_delta, the upper-ratio requirement1535, and sufficiently small coupling.
