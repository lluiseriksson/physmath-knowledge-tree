A. COMPLETE BOTTLENECK AND THE REFERENCE — SUPPORTED

A1. The map is a complete identity, not a claim that a receiver can discard its reference. For a single input/reference state, heterodyne on the input defines an operator-valued conditional state measure T_MR(dx), with X=alpha/sqrt(E). The simulated joint state has the form

  T_MR(dx) W^-_(E,nu)(dy|x),
  W^-_(E,nu)(y|x)=integral phi_(v-|a|^2/E)(y-a x) dnu(a,v).

Retaining T_MR(dx), then preparing |sqrt(E)y><sqrt(E)y|, produces exactly the original output/reference state. The channel equality follows from the dependency's heterodyne representation with thermal preparation variance vE-|a|^2, followed by the positive coherent-state representation of that thermal state. Equality of the normal channels on all trace-class inputs extends to a tensor identity on a reference; it is not restricted to product states. The requirement E>=2K/ell gives v-|a|^2/E>=ell/2>0. The physicality threshold is satisfied as well.

A2. The unconditional MR marginal is unchanged by this nonselective local measurement. Conditional MR states generally do depend on x: that steering is essential and is retained. For the resulting classical X,Y and quantum MR, Y is produced independently of MR conditional on X. Applying the preparation channel x -> rho_MR^x to the X side of the classical joint law gives, by relative-entropy data processing,

  I(MR;Y) <= I(X;Y).

This uses quantum mutual information for the first term. It does not require M and R to be independent. The chain rule gives

  I(M;RY)-I(M;R) = I(M;Y|R)
                        = I(MR;Y)-I(R;Y) <= I(X;Y).

Preparation of B from Y, while retaining R, then gives the same upper bound for I(M;RB)-I(M;R).

A3. There is no hidden subtraction of infinite entropies in the intended finite-cost use. Define quantum mutual information by relative entropy, and use the chain rule in that sense. For finite M, both message-information terms are at most H(M). With P=E|X|^2 finite, the minus kernel obeys I(X;Y)<=log(1+2KP/ell), by revealing the independent branch only for an upper bound. Thus I(MR;Y) and I(R;Y) are finite as well. One need not assume a finite entropy for all of Bob's local memory or classical transcript. This justifies the displayed differences without manipulating S(R) separately. The same reasoning uses the finite block information bound for X^m,Y^m.

A4. The moment identity is exact: integral |alpha|^2 Q_rho(alpha) d^2alpha = Tr(rho n)+1, and for joint heterodyne of correlated inputs the total increment is m. The extra normalized cost is therefore 1/E per mode, not a dimension-dependent entanglement penalty.

Boundary: the operational claim audited here starts with arbitrary finite-dimensional preshared entanglement per block, as stated. The proof of A is dimension-free for the normal reference states that occur, including bosonic receiver memory. No separate capacity theorem for a differently defined unrestricted infinite-resource assistance model is being certified. General entanglement-breaking channels can have an EA advantage; the argument depends on this specific noisy classical factor and its cost.

B. NO-FEEDBACK EA COMPOUND CONVERSE — SUPPORTED

B1. Message-dependent trace-preserving encoding on Alice's half leaves rho_MR=pi_M tensor rho_R for the receiver's initial reference. Without feedback, the entire encoded block and its heterodyne law are independent of nu. Joint decoding with R is still permitted. Applying A to the whole block gives

  I(M;B^m R) <= I(M;Y^m R) <= I(X^m;Y^m).

The second inequality would not follow by simply discarding R; it follows from A and I(M;R)=0.

B2. For each actual law, the conditional classical kernel is memoryless. The exact relative-entropy chain identity is

  sum_i I(X_i;Y_i) = I(X^m;Y^m)
                    + D(P_(Y^m) || product_i P_(Y_i)).

Thus correlations in the Husimi input do not spoil the upper bound. With mu_bar=(1/m)sum_i mu_i, simultaneous input concavity gives sum_i I_mu_i(W^-_nu)<=m I_mu_bar(W^-_nu). This mu_bar is common to all nu and has cost at most 1+1/E. One must take inf_nu after forming this common law. Fano with the worst-law error then gives the candidate's V^-_(E,Gamma)(1+1/E) converse. No maximum dimension of the entanglement resource enters the bound or its vanishing-error limit.

B3. The dependency's noise lemma gives uniformly over every finite-cost input and every nu

  |I_mu(W^-_(E,nu))-I_mu(W_nu)| <= 4K/(ell E),
  |I_mu(W^+_(E,nu))-I_mu(W_nu)| <= 2/(ell E).

The cost value is concave, nondecreasing, zero at P=0, and bounded by log(1+KP/ell); hence V_Gamma(1+1/E)<=V_Gamma(1)+log(1+K/ell)/E. The same explicit interval therefore follows, including the stated EA-minus-unassisted upper bound [log(1+K/ell)+(4K+2)/ell]/E. The lower bound uses an allowed unassisted code, not an assumed EA coding theorem in infinite dimensions. The claim is a vanishing-error weak converse; no strong converse has been proved here.

C. ADAPTIVE FEEDBACK CONVERSE AND FINITE TRAINING — SUPPORTED WITH WORDING CORRECTION

C1. Track the actual protocol at a fixed nu. Before each forward use, let R_i contain all of Bob's retained quantum memory, past classical information and feedback copies. Alice's operations, even controlled by M and the received transcript, do not change the MR_i marginal: they are trace preserving locally for each message. Apply the complete identity only to the next channel use, obtain the actual post-channel state by preparing B_i from Y_i, and use A:

  I(M;R_i B_i)-I(M;R_i) <= I(X_i;Y_i).

This is a comparison of the actual information increment. It is unnecessary to give an enhanced receiver permanent access to all hidden simulation variables or to alter the subsequent protocol. Bob's local instrument, retaining its outcome, is a channel on his existing system. Copying a classical feedback outcome and sending that copy to Alice cannot increase I(M;Bob). Summing the increments, with initial I(M;R_0)=0, proves the candidate's displayed telescope.

The original line 75 uses “accessible information.” That phrase, if understood as the supremum of classical information over measurements, is not justified by A. Optimized accessible information does not have the quantum chain rule used here; quantum side information can be unlocked by an additional classical register. The displayed proof concerns quantum I(M;R), not an increment of optimized accessible information. AUTHOR-CLARIFICATION744a explicitly adopts that correction. With this correction, there is no remaining chain-rule objection to the displayed capacity bound.

C2. Put P_i(nu)=E_nu |X_i|^2, averaging over the uniform message, channel history and all protocol randomness. The heterodyne identity and the required actual-law photon constraint give

  sum_i P_i(nu) <= m(1+1/E).

The law of X_i may depend on nu through feedback; this is allowed. Use only the individual fixed-law capacity and its concavity:

  sum_i I(X_i;Y_i) <= sum_i C^-_(E,nu)(P_i(nu))
                    <= m C^-_(E,nu)(1+1/E).

There is no demand that each use or each history have cost <=E. Nonnegative costs and a finite total expectation make each P_i finite. The noise and cost estimates yield C_nu(1)+[log(1+K/ell)+4K/ell]/E for each nu. Fano followed by inf_nu is therefore legitimate. A budget averaged over a prior on nu would not suffice; the per-law hypothesis is indispensable and correctly stated.

C3. The finite-family lower bound is sound at each fixed E. For each pair of distinct coherent/heterodyne kernels choose a finite x and Borel output bin on which the probabilities differ. A finite family needs only finitely many such experiments. Positive allocations of t_m probes to these experiments, with t_m->infinity and t_m/m->0, distinguish their finite probability vectors by bounded-observation concentration. Identical kernels can be grouped for this direct construction. The pilot is independent of M. Bob sends the estimated index once and both parties use the corresponding decoder/codebook.

C4. A fixed rate below min_nu C^+_(E,nu)(1-delta) can be implemented with the same message count and payload length for every index, because the family is finite. The full coding proposition in the dependency supplies deterministic finite-alphabet codes with per-word cost slack, which is stronger than necessary here. A selected wrong-index codebook still obeys the same budget. Even if only its uniform-message average budget were used, selection is message independent, so averaging over the selected index preserves that budget. If Q is the finite average pilot input cost, t_m Q+(m-t_m)(1-delta)<=m eventually. Thus the pilot cost is absorbed without a hidden high-cost error event.

C5. Uniform error is bounded by identification error plus the matching payload error, and both vanish. First fix E, delta, probes, finite approximations and rate slack; then let blocklength grow. Finally remove delta by continuity of the finite concave capacity functions at P=1. Only after obtaining the capacities at fixed E is E taken large. There is no interchange of a uniform-in-E coding limit, no direct theorem for an infinite compound family, and no claimed finite-length efficiency.

D. STRICT MATCHED-RESET/AWGN GAP — SUPPORTED

D1. The two laws belong to one allowed parameter rectangle with K=k, ell=r, U=s=k+r:

  nu_1=lambda delta_(sqrt(k),r)+q delta_(0,s),
  nu_2=delta_(b,r), q=1-lambda.

The reset variance is s, not r. This matching is responsible for the output phi_s under X~CN(0,1). For k,r>0 and 0<lambda<1, the reset channel is nonconstant in x, so a two-point input of sufficiently small positive cost already gives strictly positive mutual information. Its capacity C1 is finite since revealing the independent flag gives C1<=lambda log(1+k/r).

D2. Attainment and continuity are genuine, not merely asserted. Let B={mu: integral |x|^2 dmu<=1}. Markov's inequality gives tightness; lower semicontinuity of the second moment makes B weakly closed. Prokhorov's theorem therefore gives weak compactness on the plane. If mu_n converges weakly to mu in B, each output density converges pointwise: for fixed y, the Gaussian kernel is a bounded continuous function of x. The densities have integral one, so Scheffe's lemma gives L1 convergence.

All these output densities are bounded by 1/(pi r) and have one common second-moment bound. For W1 the second moment is lambda k E|X|^2+lambda r+q s<=s; for W2 it is b^2 E|X|^2+r<=b^2+r. The dependency's entropy lemma is applicable. Its key tail estimate is valid: if delta is the mass outside radius R and the second moment is <=M, then

  -delta log L <= -integral_tail f log f
                <= delta log(pi e M/delta^2),  delta<=M/R^2.

Both sides tend uniformly to zero as R grows, while bounded-density entropy is continuous on each fixed ball. A uniform second moment here is enough; moment convergence and uniform integrability of |X|^2 are not required.

D3. For W1, with F the signal/reset flag,

  h(W1(.|x)) = lambda log(pi e r)+q log(pi e s)
               + h2(lambda)-H(F|Y,X=x).

The last term is bounded between 0 and h2(lambda). It is continuous in x: Gaussian translation gives L1 convergence locally, with bounded local moments, and the same entropy argument applies. Thus conditional entropy is bounded continuous and its input average converges weakly. W2 has constant conditional entropy log(pi e r). Together with output entropy continuity, this proves continuity of I1 and I2 throughout B. It proves attainment of C1 and of max_B min(I1,I2).

D4. The dependency's non-Gaussian improvement was checked, rather than inferred from the title of its proposition. Under mu0=CN(0,1), the output is exactly phi_s. H(x)=H(F|Y,X=x) is radial, strictly positive at finite x, bounded, and tends to zero for |x|->infinity by separation of the two Gaussian clouds and binary Fano. It is not affine in |x|^2. Two disjoint radial annuli provide independent mass/moment correctors, hence a bounded compactly supported radial h with integral h dmu0=integral |x|^2 h dmu0=0 and a0=integral hH dmu0>0. For sufficiently small positive epsilon, mu_epsilon=(1+epsilon h)mu0 is feasible, and

  I_mu_epsilon(W1)-I_mu0(W1)
       =epsilon a0-D(p_Y,epsilon || phi_s)
       >=epsilon a0-epsilon^2 integral h^2 dmu0 >0.

The exact identity uses the fixed output second moment s and the flag entropy identity; the quadratic bound is KL data processing followed by log(1+u)<=u. This establishes G1<C1 with no numeric approximation.

D5. Define b^2=r(exp(C1)-1). Positivity of C1 gives b>0; the flag upper bound gives

  b^2 <= r[(1+k/r)^lambda-1] < k.

For W2, h(Y)<=log(pi e E|Y|^2)<=log(pi e(b^2+r)). Equality under the input cost bound requires Y~CN(0,b^2+r), including zero mean and circular covariance. Since its additive Gaussian characteristic function never vanishes and b is nonzero, deconvolution uniquely forces X~CN(0,1). Thus C2=log(1+b^2/r)=C1 with that unique maximizing input. If the attained common minimum equalled C1, its maximizing mu would have to be this unique W2 optimizer; but its W1 information is G1<C1. Therefore Delta=C1-V_{1,2}(1)>0.

D6. Distinguishability at fixed E has an especially simple zero-photon proof. The plus kernel at x=0 under law 1 is lambda phi_(r+1/E)+q phi_(s+1/E); under law 2 it is phi_(r+1/E). Put u_E=r+1/E, w_E=s+1/E. For Z=1{|Y|^2<=u_E}, the probabilities in the author supplement are

  p2=1-exp(-1),
  p1=lambda(1-exp(-1))+q(1-exp(-u_E/w_E)),
  Delta_E=p2-p1=q[exp(-u_E/w_E)-exp(-1)]>0.

These follow from the complex Gaussian radial exponential law. The midpoint decision on t independent bits has each conditional error <=exp(-t Delta_E^2/2) by the one-sided Hoeffding inequality. Vacuum probes have exactly zero transmitted photon cost. For fixed E, t=ceil(A log m), with any fixed A>0, has vanishing error and t/m->0. One bit of feedback selects the payload. The addendum's explicit threshold, constant and limit order are correct. This observation is within the current audit, not a separate review.

D7. Put A0=log(1+k/r)+4k/r and B0=2/r. Combining the proved bounds gives

  C_unassisted,FB(E,{nu1,nu2})-C_EA,noFB(E,{nu1,nu2})
          >= Delta-(A0+B0)/E.

The difference is strictly positive whenever E>=2k/r and E>(A0+B0)/Delta. This is an existential threshold because Delta and C1 were not numerically computed. No single-channel ordering between entanglement and feedback is implied. It compares protocols with different resources on an unknown fixed law.
