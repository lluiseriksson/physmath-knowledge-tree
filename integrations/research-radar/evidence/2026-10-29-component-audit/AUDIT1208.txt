1. SPECTRAL DIMENSION BOUND — SUPPORTED

Use D_tr(rho,sigma) = ||rho-sigma||_1/2. Assume Psi:M_d -> M_d is CPTP and eta_tr(Psi) <= r, where eta_tr is the supremum of the output/input trace-distance ratio over distinct states. Take r >= 0; its meaningful range is [0,1]. Define osc(H)=lambda_max(H)-lambda_min(H). No unitality of Psi is assumed or needed.

For Hermitian trace-zero Delta,
  |Tr(H Delta)| <= osc(H) ||Delta||_1/2.
Indeed subtract ((lambda_max(H)+lambda_min(H))/2) I and apply Holder's inequality. Let u,v be extremal orthogonal eigenvectors of G=Psi*(F), when osc(G)>0. Their input trace distance is one, so
  osc(G) = Tr[F(Psi(|u><u|)-Psi(|v><v|))]
         <= osc(F) D_tr(Psi(|u><u|),Psi(|v><v|))
         <= r osc(F).
If osc(G)=0 the assertion is immediate. Thus the normalization has no missing factor two.

Writing the eigenvalues of G as lambda_min(G) plus nonnegative increments, at most d-1 of which can be nonzero, gives
  lambda_min(G) >= Tr(G)/d - (d-1) osc(G)/d.
For the given positive POVM density F_a with integral F_a dnu=I, positivity implies osc(F_a)<=Tr(F_a), and integral Tr(F_a) dnu=d. Because Psi* is unital,
  integral G_a dnu=I,    integral Tr(G_a) dnu=d.
Integrating the previous inequalities proves
  b = integral lambda_min(G_a) dnu >= 1-(d-1)r.
Also 0<=c_a=lambda_min(G_a)<=Tr(G_a)/d, so 0<=b<=1. Therefore
  b >= max(0,1-(d-1)r).
The same d appears as both prefix input and output dimension, exactly as requested. This proof is not a claim about arbitrary rectangular prefixes with the same constant.

For d=1 the conclusion is directly b=1; the empty supremum defining eta can be assigned zero, or that case can simply be stated separately. For r=0 every G_a is scalar almost everywhere and b=1. At r=1/(d-1) the lower guarantee becomes zero, and the classical example below attains it. No positivity is claimed above that threshold.

2. MEASURABILITY, INTEGRABILITY, AND ACTUAL POSTERIORS — SUPPORTED

Entrywise measurable finite matrices are strongly measurable. Psi* is a fixed continuous linear map, and the minimum eigenvalue is a continuous function of a Hermitian matrix. Thus G_a and c_a are measurable without selecting measurable eigenvectors. The bound c_a<=Tr(G_a)/d supplies integrability. Each prepared state tau_a has trace norm one; hence c_a tau_a is integrable. Positive matrix fields are norm-integrable whenever their traces are integrable. A POVM density need not obey F_a<=I pointwise, and the proof correctly does not use that false density bound.

For the actual last branch T_a(X)=Tr(F_a X) tau_a, all positive-probability earlier records followed by a finish at a yield the same final posterior tau_a. Summing the unnormalized previous branch maps gives Psi; the intervening normalization factors cancel against the branch probabilities. Consequently the block posterior-state kernel is
  K(rho,B)=integral Tr(G_a rho) 1_B(tau_a) dnu.
Since G_a>=c_a I, for b>0 the measure
  lambda(B)=b^(-1) integral c_a 1_B(tau_a) dnu
is a probability measure, and K(rho,B)>=b lambda(B) for every state rho and Borel B. Zero-probability records do not affect the identity. The tau map may be many-to-one; no injectivity is needed.

This identity uses the stipulated last branch, fixed apart from allowed environmental parameters. A feedback protocol whose last branch also depends on preceding observed records requires its own specified representation and cannot silently be substituted. The conclusion concerns posterior states at the block end, not a common component of complete outcome histories. It also is not a statement about posteriors conditioned on a retained quantum reference.

If the appropriate prefixes have eta_tr<=q^(L-1), 0<=q<1, choose L with (d-1)q^(L-1)<1. The positive constant is uniform when that bound is uniform. Random-environment applications still require joint measurability, a measurable reference measure kernel when it varies, a common full-measure set, and the source's time-shift convention. These are retained assumptions, not results of the scalar estimate. No quenched CLT or general limit theorem was audited here.

For b=0, the normalized lambda formula is undefined; only the vacuous zero minorization is asserted. For b=1, integral Tr(G_a-c_a I)=0 forces G_a=c_a I almost everywhere. The observed final posterior law is then independent of rho. Even when b=0, the optimal minorization on posterior space can be larger, because different labels can prepare the same state.

3. CONTINUOUS CHOI REMAINDER AND ENDPOINTS — SUPPORTED

Let N=E o Psi and Omega=integral c_a tau_a dnu. Then Omega>=0 and Tr(Omega)=b. With the unnormalized input-first convention
  J(N)=sum_ij |i><j| tensor N(|i><j|),
the identity Tr(G |i><j|)=G_ji gives
  J(N)=integral G_a^T tensor tau_a dnu,
  J(R_Omega)=I tensor Omega,
  J(N-R_Omega)=integral (G_a-c_a I)^T tensor tau_a dnu.
The transpose is an ordinary basis transpose, not an adjoint. Transposition preserves positive semidefiniteness of an individual matrix. Hence each tensor-product integrand in the last expression is positive; no assertion that partial transpose preserves arbitrary positive bipartite matrices is being made.

The last integrand's trace is Tr(G_a)-d c_a>=0, whose integral equals d(1-b). Its trace norm equals its trace, so the Choi integral is a finite-dimensional Bochner integral. The integrals of G_a^T tensor tau_a and I tensor c_a tau_a are likewise well-defined. Thus exchanging finite sums and integrals in the Choi identity is justified, and N-R_Omega is CP.

For all X, Tr[(N-R_Omega)(X)]=(1-b)Tr(X). Equivalently the output partial trace of its Choi operator is (1-b)I. This is the trace-preserving normalization condition; the full Choi trace is d(1-b), not 1-b. Therefore:
  b=0: Omega=0 and Q=N is CPTP.
  0<b<1: omega=Omega/b is a state, Q=(N-R_Omega)/(1-b) is CPTP,
         N=b R_omega+(1-b)Q.
  b=1: the positive remainder has zero trace, so it vanishes and N=R_Omega.

Minor presentation repair (NEEDS CORRECTION if the display is read literally): PROOF1207 labels the decomposition display 'when b>0' although Q was defined only for b<1. State the middle case as 0<b<1 and give N=R_Omega separately at b=1, or explicitly permit arbitrary Q multiplied by zero there. The preceding prose already gives the correct endpoint argument; this is not a failure of the theorem.

The unnormalized convention differs from the normalized output-first convention in Hirche's 2024 SDP: swap factors and divide the Choi operator by d when translating. There is no missing d in the supplied convention.

Define
  alpha_+(N)=max {Tr W : W>=0, J(N)>=I tensor W}.
Omega is feasible, so alpha_+(N)>=b. This is a lower bound, not a claim that the representation optimizes alpha_+. Nor does it identify the positive coefficient with Hermitian-relaxed or merely positive-map variants for general EB outputs.

4. EQUAL-REFERENCE COMPLETE CONSEQUENCES — SUPPORTED

Let rho_AR and sigma_AR be states with the same R marginal mu_R. For 0<b<1,
  (N tensor id_R)(rho)=b omega tensor mu_R+(1-b)(Q tensor id_R)(rho),
and the analogous expression for sigma has the identical first term. Subtraction and trace-norm contractivity of a channel yield
  D_tr((N tensor id)(rho),(N tensor id)(sigma))
    <=(1-b) D_tr(rho,sigma).
This holds for every finite reference dimension with the same constant. It does not estimate an unrestricted diamond-norm contraction or comparisons with unequal reference marginals.

Joint convexity of Umegaki relative entropy, followed by data processing for Q tensor id, similarly yields
  D((N tensor id)(rho) || (N tensor id)(sigma))
    <= (1-b) D((Q tensor id)(rho) || (Q tensor id)(sigma))
    <= (1-b) D(rho||sigma).
For a ratio definition use 0<D(rho||sigma)<infinity. No full-rank assumption is needed. Infinite relative entropy is handled in the extended sense for b<1; at b=1 both output states coincide, so handle it directly instead of writing 0 times infinity. At b=0 ordinary data processing gives factor one. Combining with section 1 gives the claimed upper factor min(1,(d-1)r).

Smallest useful scope falsifier if equal marginals are removed: take any replacer N and product inputs with the same A state but different qubit R states. Then N tensor id preserves their trace distance and relative entropy. For example choose reference distributions (1/2,1/2) and (3/4,1/4) to keep relative entropy finite and positive. The claimed factor zero at b=1 would be false. Thus equality of the R marginals is essential.

6. CLASSICAL FAMILY AND THE EXACT ENVELOPE — SUPPORTED

For d>=2 and 0<=s<=1, let
  P_s(y|i)=(1-s)/d+s(1-delta_yi)/(d-1),
and let Psi_s dephase the input and output this distribution in the standard d-state basis. It is CPTP and entanglement breaking. For any Hermitian trace-zero Delta,
  Psi_s(Delta)=-(s/(d-1)) Diag(Delta).
Dephasing is trace-norm contractive, so eta_tr(Psi_s)<=s/(d-1), including for arbitrary coherent quantum inputs. Two different basis states attain equality. This fills the step from the checker's classical row-pair calculation to the full quantum ordinary trace coefficient.

For each y, min_i P_s(y|i)=(1-s)/d. Thus the common diagonal operator W=(1-s)I/d is feasible and has trace 1-s. Conversely, if W is any positive CP common component, testing J(Psi_s)-I tensor W on each |i> tensor |y> gives W_yy<=P_s(y|i). Summing the resulting W_yy<=(1-s)/d yields Tr W<=1-s, even if W has off-diagonal entries. Therefore alpha_+(Psi_s)=1-s. E may be the output dephasing map, so E o Psi_s=Psi_s and E is admissible.

Let F_d(r) be the infimum of alpha_+(E o Psi) over the stipulated square d-dimensional prefixes with eta_tr(Psi)<=r and finite-dimensional EB postprocessors, allowing d orthogonal output states. Section 3 supplies
  F_d(r)>=max(0,1-(d-1)r).
For 0<=r<=1/(d-1), choose s=(d-1)r; the explicit pair realizes equality. For r>=1/(d-1), choose s=1; its coefficient is zero and its eta_tr=1/(d-1)<=r. Hence
  F_d(r)=max(0,1-(d-1)r),
and the infimum is attained. The natural contraction range is 0<=r<=1; an upper bound r>1 merely repeats the zero plateau. At d=1 the separate envelope is one. Padding the output permits any fixed output dimension at least d. No assertion for a fixed smaller output dimension follows from this witness.

This is sharpness of the common-component envelope under a <=r constraint. It is not sharpness of either complete-contraction envelope; it does not even supply lower bounds on those coefficients. A tight sufficient certificate is not generally a tight data-processing coefficient. The separate future binary-symmetric-channel sketch was not read or audited.

The classical lower comparison itself is elementary from the known common-component formula. For d distributions P_i,
  1-sum_y min_i P_i(y)
   =sum_y max_i(P_1(y)-P_i(y))
   <=sum_{i=2}^d sum_y(P_1(y)-P_i(y))_+
   =sum_{i=2}^d TV(P_1,P_i)
   <=(d-1) eta_TV.
The first maximum includes i=1, so it is nonnegative. This also explains why the comparison should not be presented as a newly invented classical coefficient theorem.

7. ADVERSE CONTROLS AND PROHIBITED INFERENCES

(a) Arbitrary recorded instruments: REFUTED. The four-Pauli qubit instrument T_u(rho)=U rho U*/4 has a completely replacing summed channel. The posterior chain fixes I/2, and it also leaves the uniform law on the two computational pure states stationary. The laws have equal barycenter and disjoint supports. From either support, no positive common posterior minorization across both starting states can exist, even after a finite block. A one-outcome preparation of I/2 has the same nonselective channel but a different posterior kernel. This is already a dimension-two falsifier; a one-dimensional system has only one state.

(b) Posterior TV convergence from trace/Wasserstein convergence: REFUTED. The one-record map rho -> rho/2+I/4 gives distinct deterministic posterior states from the two basis inputs at every finite time L. Their point-mass laws have TV distance one; their state trace distance and trace-metric W1 distance are 2^(-L). The kernel converges weakly/Wasserstein toward I/2 while TV convergence from those nonstationary starting states fails.

(c) Identification of b with an optimized channel or posterior coefficient: REFUTED in general. Take d=2, Psi=id, F_0=|0><0|, F_1=|1><1|, and tau_0=tau_1=tau. Then b=0, yet E o Psi is a replacer with alpha_+=1 and the actual posterior law is always delta_tau. Thus b is a valid feasible certificate whose value can depend on the representation and on retaining labels before pushing forward to states.

(d) Removing the EB postprocessor and claiming the same CP component for Psi: REFUTED. A useful prior qubit example is Phi(X)=(Tr(X)I+X^T)/3. Its eta_tr is 1/3, but J(Phi)=(I+Swap)/3 has symmetric support and alpha_+(Phi)=0. A nonzero I tensor W cannot have its support contained in that symmetric subspace: choose u in supp W and v perpendicular to u. This is the d=2 specialization of the checked source example in Pathirana, Appendix A, Example 4. Taking E=id would incorrectly predict at least 2/3. Identity is not an admissible EB postprocessor here. The counterexample is a boundary check, not a contradiction to the audited theorem.

(e) Positive versus Hermitian-relaxed components: NOT IDENTIFIED for general EB output by this proof. Positivity of Omega is essential to the probability-mixture and entropy arguments. A feasible arbitrary Hermitian W cannot simply be substituted for a state.

(f) Worldwide novelty, sharp complete coefficients, capacity formulas, and a full random-environment theorem: NOT ESTABLISHED. Finite controls do not certify these assertions or replace the analytic proof.
