# Reciprocal transformation is not sufficient for persistent oscillation

The yin-yang image motivates a model-selection question: can a dominant regime generate a measurable reserve or delayed response that later reverses it? It supplies no physical equation or evidence that nature lacks equilibria. These are known-model checks and an original research protocol, not a new theorem, natural law or separate paper.

## Two obstructions and a model that can cycle

For a,b>0, positive interconversion gives p'=a(1-p)-bp. Its exact solution is p(t)=a/(a+b)+(p(0)-a/(a+b)) exp(-(a+b)t). Both directions remain active while the fraction relaxes. Reciprocal generation is therefore insufficient for macroscopic cycling.

More generally, an autonomous locally Lipschitz scalar ODE on a real interval has no nonconstant periodic solution. A periodic solution attains an extremum; its derivative there is zero, so the extremum is an equilibrium. Uniqueness prevents departure. This excludes neither circle-valued phases nor nonautonomous, discrete or stochastic systems.

A familiar illustrative normal form uses signed states d,s:

    d'=(mu-d²-s²)d-omega*s
    s'=omega*d+(mu-d²-s²)s

For r>0 this gives r'=mu*r-r³ and theta'=omega. For mu>0, omega!=0 and nonzero initial state, r approaches sqrt(mu), giving persistent rotation on the attracting circle. For mu<0 the radius decays. Here s is a signed response, not a positive amount or independently established physical force. Renaming the known normal form is not discovery. ## A positive reservoir model exposes what the fraction loses

Consider the standard dimensionless Brusselator, with A>0 and B>=0:

    x'=A-(B+1)x+x²y
    y'=Bx-x²y

The positive quadrant is forward invariant: x=0 gives x'=A and y=0 gives y'=Bx. The flux Bx converts x to y; x²y returns y to x autocatalytically and requires x already present. Constant feed A and the x drain make the dictionary asymmetric. A literal internal-seed-only account would omit a necessary model input. Gallas's selected equations(1)–(4) supply this model context; the later externally forced study is not being reproduced.

The unique positive equilibrium is (A,B/A). Its Jacobian is [[B-1,A²],[-B,-A²]], with trace B-1-A² and determinant A². It is linearly asymptotically stable below B=1+A² and unstable above. Pure imaginary eigenvalues at the boundary alone do not prove a nonlinear Hopf branch or its stability. No such proof is claimed here.

For c=x+y>0 and p=x/c, direct differentiation yields

    c'=A-x
    p'=A(1-p)/c-(B+1)p+p²+p²(1-p)c².

At A=1,B=3,p=1/2, c=2 gives p'=-1, whereas c=4 gives p'=3/8. Thus identical visible proportions have opposite immediate directions. A single function p'=F(p) cannot represent both states. The missing total is independently measurable; it is not inferred by defining a seed from the future derivative. This is a known-model state-reduction counterexample, not a universal mechanism of yin-yang.

Set A=0. Then c'=-x<=0. A nonnegative periodic trajectory would satisfy integral x dt=0 over its period; continuity forces x identically zero, hence y constant. Sustained nonconstant cycling is impossible in this un-fed model. The argument eliminates this particular supply-free route; it is not a general thermodynamic theorem and says nothing against transients or other supplied systems.
